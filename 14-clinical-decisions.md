This chapter brings together the "when, how and what next" of CT coronary angiography. It guides you through vetting requests, optimising acquisitions, classifying findings reproducibly, and linking those findings to clear management decisions. The aim is a report that answers "what next?" as confidently as "what is there?"

{% tab chapter-14 Pre-Scan Decisions %}

## Where CTCA Sits in the Pathway

- **First-line** for stable chest pain without known CAD.
- Clarification of equivocal or discordant functional tests.
- Reassessment of known non-obstructive CAD with new symptoms.
- Pre-procedural planning (TAVI, structural intervention, graft assessment).
- Suspected anomalous coronary arteries — best non-invasive anatomical test.

**Not the right test** for high-risk ACS (ongoing ischaemia, dynamic ECG, rising troponin) — these need invasive pathways.

## Contraindications and Cautions

- True iodinated contrast anaphylaxis — premedicate or use alternative test.
- <<eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² or acute kidney injury: defer if possible.>>
- Pregnancy: avoid unless life-saving and MDT-agreed.
- **Beta-blocker contraindications:** severe asthma/bronchospasm, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block, <<resting HR <50 bpm, SBP <100 mmHg>>, decompensated heart failure.
- **Nitrate contraindications:** <<sildenafil or vardenafil within 24 hours; tadalafil within 48 hours>>; SBP <100–110 mmHg; severe AS or HOCM (caution).

## Pre-Scan Optimisation

- **Heart rate target:** <<≤60 bpm and regular for prospective ECG-triggered or high-pitch helical acquisition>> (accept ≤65 bpm if very regular).
- **Beta-blockade:** oral metoprolol 50–100 mg 1–2 hours pre-scan; IV metoprolol 5–20 mg titrated if needed. Alternatives: ivabradine, diltiazem (caution).
- **Sublingual GTN:** <<400–800 micrograms 2–5 minutes pre-acquisition>> unless contraindicated.
- **Contrast:** typically 60–80 mL at 5–6 mL/s plus 30–50 mL saline chaser; tailor kVp (70–100 in slim, 100–120 in larger patients).
- **Metformin:** do not routinely stop if eGFR ≥30 and no AKI risk; withhold and restart at 48 hours if eGFR <30.

## Acquisition Mode Selection

- **High-pitch helical:** HR ≤60 bpm, very regular; very low dose.
- **Prospective step-and-shoot:** HR ≤60–65 bpm, regular; low dose, excellent quality.
- **Retrospective gating:** irregular rhythm (AF, ectopy), HR >70–75 bpm, or when functional assessment needed; higher dose.

## Stenosis Grading (Diameter Reduction)

| Grade | Stenosis |
|---|---|
| Minimal | <25% |
| Mild | 25–49% |
| Moderate | 50–69% |
| Severe | ≥70% |
| Left main severe | <<≥50%>> |

## CAD-RADS 2.0 and Next Steps

- **CAD-RADS 0** (no plaque): reassure; consider non-cardiac cause.
- **CAD-RADS 1** (1–24%): lifestyle and risk-factor optimisation; consider statin.
- **CAD-RADS 2** (25–49%): preventive therapy; no further anatomical testing routinely.
- **CAD-RADS 3** (50–69%): <<consider CT-FFR, stress perfusion, or ICA depending on vessel and symptoms.>>
- **CAD-RADS 4A** (70–99% in 1–2 vessels): ICA for revascularisation consideration.
- **CAD-RADS 4B:** <<left main ≥50% or three-vessel severe stenoses — urgent cardiology/MDT and ICA.>>
- **CAD-RADS 5** (total occlusion): ICA referral; CTO pathway if symptomatic/viable.
- **CAD-RADS N** (non-diagnostic): alternative functional test or ICA based on risk.

**Modifiers:** S (stent), G (graft), V (vulnerable plaque), I (ischaemia from CT-FFR/CTP), E (extracardiac findings).

## CT-FFR (FFRCT) Thresholds

- <<≤0.80: haemodynamically significant.>>
- 0.76–0.80: borderline ("grey zone") — integrate with symptoms and territory.
- >0.80: not haemodynamically significant.
- **When to request:** intermediate stenosis (40–90%) in vessels ≥2.0–2.5 mm; unreliable with heavy blooming or very small/distal vessels.

## High-Risk Plaque Features

- <<Low attenuation plaque: <30 HU.>>
- <<Positive remodelling index >1.1.>>
- Spotty calcification: <3 mm nodular foci.
- Napkin-ring sign.

Presence supports aggressive preventive therapy and lowers the threshold for functional testing or ICA.

## Calcium Score (Agatston)

| Score | Category | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| <<0>> | None | Very low likelihood; in symptomatic patients still proceed to CTCA to exclude non-calcified plaque |
| 1–99 | Mild | Atheroma present; optimise risk factors |
| 100–399 | Moderate | Higher obstructive likelihood; consider CT-FFR for intermediate lesions |
| <<≥400>> | Severe | High multivessel risk and reduced CTCA specificity due to blooming; consider functional testing or ICA |

{% tab chapter-14 Turning Findings into Plans %}

## Worked Examples

- **Normal coronaries (CAD-RADS 0):** No CT evidence of CAD. Consider non-cardiac causes. No further cardiac testing unless symptoms persist/worsen.
- **Non-obstructive CAD with high-risk plaque:** initiate/intensify high-intensity statin aiming ≥40% LDL-C reduction; optimise BP, diabetes, lifestyle.
- **Intermediate proximal LAD stenosis (CAD-RADS 3):** request CT-FFR; if ≤0.80 → ICA; if >0.80 → optimal medical therapy.
- **Severe left main (CAD-RADS 4B):** <<urgent cardiology/heart-team discussion and ICA the same day.>>
- **Non-diagnostic stented RCA (<3.0 mm):** recommend stress perfusion CMR or ICA depending on urgency.

## Same-Day Escalation Triggers

- <<Left main stenosis ≥50%.>>
- <<Proximal LAD stenosis ≥70% with correlating symptoms.>>
- Three-vessel severe disease.
- CAD-RADS 5 (acute occlusion) in symptomatic patient.
- CT-FFR ≤0.80 in a major territory.
- Acute aortic pathology or pulmonary embolism on source images.

## Preventive Therapy Prompts

- **Statins:** if any atheroma is present, recommend lipid optimisation (e.g. atorvastatin 20–80 mg).
- **Antiplatelets:** not routinely indicated for primary prevention based on CTCA alone. In known CAD with prior MI/PCI, follow cardiology plan.
- Flag modifiable risks (BP, diabetes, smoking, weight) for GP/cardiology follow-up.

## Special Scenarios

- **Stents:** diagnostic confidence best ≥3.0 mm; if non-diagnostic and symptoms correlate, recommend stress imaging or ICA.
- **CABG:** CTCA excels at graft patency; refer occluded/stenotic grafts with symptoms.
- **Anomalous coronaries:** <<inter-arterial course between aorta and pulmonary artery, slit-like ostium, acute take-off, or intramural course are high-risk features warranting specialist/MDT review.>>
- **Myocardial bridging:** <<beta-blockers for symptoms; avoid nitrates — they may worsen symptoms by reducing diastolic perfusion.>>
- **Suspected SCAD:** CTCA sensitivity is variable; involve cardiology early; ICA may still be required.

## Communication and Documentation

- Always document limitations (motion, calcification, stents).
- Use a structured report: CAD-RADS category and modifiers, per-vessel stenosis, and explicit management recommendation with urgency.
- Phone the referrer for time-critical findings and record the conversation.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Overcalling calcified lesions:** blooming exaggerates stenosis. Use multiple kernels, thin slices, and orthogonal views; be cautious in <3.0 mm vessels. Consider CT-FFR or functional testing.
- **Ignoring left main and ostia:** always start there — small ostial plaques matter.
- **Equating "any plaque" with "needs stent":** management is symptom- and ischaemia-driven; most non-obstructive CAD needs preventive therapy, not revascularisation.
- **Skipping rate/rhythm optimisation:** motion is the commonest reason for non-diagnostic scans.
- **Missing high-risk plaque:** systematically check for low attenuation, positive remodelling, napkin-ring.
- **Not giving a plan:** end every report with clear next steps and urgency.
- **Nitrates after PDE-5 inhibitors:** <<always ask about sildenafil/vardenafil (24 h) and tadalafil (48 h) before giving GTN.>>
- **Under-communicating urgent findings:** call the referrer for left main, severe proximal LAD, or CT-FFR ≤0.80.

## Pearls

- **Vet every request:** stable? Likely diagnostic? Right question? If "no" to any, redirect.
- **Invest in rate control:** five minutes with metoprolol saves a repeat scan.
- **Integrate CT-FFR with anatomy:** a borderline 0.76–0.80 in a proximal LAD supplying viable myocardium often warrants ICA; the same value in a small distal vessel may not.
- **Use modifiers actively:** flagging V (vulnerable plaque) or E (extracardiac finding) changes downstream care.
- **Heavy calcium (Agatston ≥400):** consider whether CTCA will answer the question before scanning — functional testing or ICA may be more efficient.
- **Bridging vs SCAD vs anomaly:** subtle distinctions, big management differences. Read the proximal anatomy carefully.

## Key Take-Aways

- CTCA is a decision-making test — your report must answer "what next?" not just "what is there?"
- Optimise heart rate (<<≤60 bpm>>) and give sublingual GTN before acquiring; motion and missed vasodilation are the commonest causes of non-diagnostic scans.
- Use CAD-RADS 2.0 with modifiers for reproducible categorisation; tie each category to an explicit management recommendation.
- <<CT-FFR ≤0.80 indicates haemodynamic significance>> and supports ICA referral when in a major territory supplying viable myocardium.
- Escalate urgently for <<left main ≥50%, proximal LAD ≥70%, three-vessel severe disease, or acute total occlusion with viable territory>>.
- Recognise when CTCA will not be diagnostic (Agatston ≥400, small stents, severe arrhythmia) and recommend the right alternative promptly.
