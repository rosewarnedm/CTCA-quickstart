Coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring is a fast, low-dose, non-contrast ECG-gated CT technique that quantifies calcified coronary atherosclerosis. It is highly reproducible and strongly prognostic for future cardiovascular events, and it plays a key role both as a stand-alone test and as preparation for CTCA.

{% tab chapter-7 Clinical Role %}

## What CAC scoring tells you

- Quantifies the burden of calcified coronary plaque in Agatston units (AU).
- Marker of total coronary atherosclerotic burden and future event risk.
- Does **not** detect non-calcified plaque and does **not** directly assess stenosis severity.

## Where it fits (UK practice)

- **Symptomatic stable chest pain:** NICE recommends CTCA as first-line. <<CAC alone should not be used to diagnose CAD or to rule out disease in symptomatic patients.>>
- **Pre-CTCA planning:** Anticipates blooming artefact and informs protocol selection.
- **Primary prevention:** Refines risk in borderline/intermediate 10-year risk where the statin decision is uncertain. <<In the UK, aspirin is not recommended for primary prevention.>>
- **Incidental CAC on non-gated chest CT:** Report qualitatively (none/mild/moderate/severe) to trigger risk factor optimisation.

## Indications

- Pre-CTCA calcium score to inform protocol/interpretation.
- Borderline/intermediate cardiovascular risk in asymptomatic adults.
- Family history of premature CAD or suspected familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH).

## Contraindications and limitations

- Relative: pregnancy; inability to breath-hold; uncontrolled arrhythmia.
- Does **not** exclude non-calcified plaque (particularly in younger patients/smokers).
- Heavy calcification reduces diagnostic accuracy of subsequent CTCA.

## Agatston method (standard)

A calcified lesion is defined as:

- <<Attenuation ≥130 HU in at least 1 voxel, AND area ≥1.0 mm² (≥3 contiguous pixels on a 512×512 matrix)>>, on
- <<ECG-gated, non-contrast images acquired at 120 kVp with 2.5–3.0 mm contiguous slices.>>

**Per-focus score = lesion area (mm²) × density weighting factor:**

| Peak HU | Density factor |
|---|---|
| <<130–199>> | <<1>> |
| <<200–299>> | <<2>> |
| <<300–399>> | <<3>> |
| <<≥400>> | <<4>> |

Per-vessel scores (LM, LAD, LCx, RCA) are summed for the total Agatston score.

## Other quantitative measures

- **Volume score (mm³):** less sensitive to noise; useful for follow-up.
- **Mass score (mg HA):** most reproducible; requires calibration phantom.

## Acquisition protocol

- **Gating:** Prospective step-and-shoot; diastolic target ~70–80% R–R (40–50% if tachycardic).
- **Tube voltage:** <<120 kVp — do not vary if comparing to reference percentiles or serial scans.>>
- **Slice thickness:** <<2.5–3.0 mm contiguous (commonly 3.0 mm).>>
- **Kernel:** Medium/soft tissue.
- **FOV:** Small cardiac FOV centred on the heart.
- **Range:** Carina to below cardiac apex.
- **Heart rate:** Beta-blockers not routinely required; aim HR <75 bpm if feasible.
- **Nitrates:** Not used.
- **Breath-hold:** 6–10 s end-inspiration, avoid Valsalva.
- **Dose:** CTDIvol ~1–3 mGy; DLP 15–60 mGy·cm; effective dose ~0.2–1.0 mSv.

## Step-by-step on the workstation

1. Confirm non-contrast, 120 kVp, 2.5–3 mm, gated reconstruction ~75% R–R.
2. Launch CAC application with <<Agatston threshold fixed at 130 HU>>.
3. Seed detection — automated with manual verification.
4. **Include** foci along LM, LAD (main vessel), LCx (main vessel), RCA to crux.
5. **Exclude:** aortic valve/annulus, mitral annular calcification, aortic wall, pericardium, myocardium, papillary muscles, lymph nodes, stents, sternal wires, prosthetic valves/grafts.
6. Carefully distinguish LM ostial calcium from aortic wall calcification on MPR.
7. Record per-vessel and total Agatston scores; generate age/sex (±ethnicity) percentile (e.g. MESA).
8. Expect inter-scan variability ~15–20%; <<changes <15% on serial scans should be interpreted with caution.>>

{% tab chapter-7 Interpretation %}

## Agatston score categories

| Total score | Category | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| <<0>> | None | Very low short/mid-term event risk; does not exclude non-calcified plaque |
| <<1–99>> | Mild | Low risk; rises with age/percentile |
| <<100–399>> | Moderate | Intermediate–high risk |
| <<≥400>> | Severe | High risk; high atherosclerotic burden |
| <<≥1000>> | Very severe | Very high risk; CTCA often non-diagnostic |

## Percentiles

- Use age/sex (±ethnicity) calculator (e.g. MESA).
- <<≥75th percentile is considered elevated and confers higher risk.>>
- In young patients, small absolute scores may still be >95th percentile and clinically important.

## Prognostic principles

- Risk is continuous; approximately doubles with each doubling of CAC.
- <<A CAC score of 0 does NOT exclude non-calcified plaque or ACS, particularly in younger patients and smokers.>>

## Decision-making pathways

**Symptomatic stable chest pain (NICE-aligned):**

- CTCA is first-line.
- CAC 0–399: CTCA generally interpretable.
- CAC 400–999: Expect reduced specificity from blooming; adjust protocol; consider alternative tests.
- <<CAC ≥1000: CTCA is frequently non-diagnostic — discuss with cardiology and consider functional imaging or invasive angiography.>>

**Asymptomatic primary prevention (pathway-dependent):**

- CAC = 0 with borderline risk: consider deferring statin; lifestyle optimisation; re-scan in 3–5 years.
- CAC 1–99: favour statin, especially if age ≥55 or ≥75th percentile.
- <<CAC ≥100 or ≥75th percentile: recommend statin therapy and aggressive risk factor modification.>>
- CAC ≥1000: refer to cardiology for comprehensive assessment.

**Follow-up:**

- CAC = 0 with borderline risk: re-scan in ~3–5 years.
- CAC >0: routine serial scanning not recommended; consider repeat after ≥3 years only if it would change management.
- Significant progression: <<annualised increase >15% or categorical shift is generally considered meaningful.>>

## Reporting

Include: acquisition details, total and per-vessel Agatston scores, risk category, age/sex percentile (with source), interpretation, and limitations (non-calcified plaque not excluded).

**Example phrasing:**

- *"Total Agatston score 176 (moderate; ~82nd percentile for age/sex). Indicates increased atherosclerotic burden and supports initiation/intensification of lipid-lowering therapy."*
- *"Total Agatston score 892 (severe). Anticipate reduced specificity on subsequent CTCA due to blooming; consider alternative testing depending on clinical context."*

## Common pitfalls

- **Mistaking non-coronary calcification for CAC:** Aortic valve/root, mitral annulus, pericardium. Trace along the expected coronary course in epicardial fat to confirm.
- **LM ostium vs aortic wall:** Use MPR; include only calcification clearly within the coronary ostium/lumen.
- **Stents:** <<Stent metal is not calcium and must be excluded; CAC does not assess in-stent restenosis.>>
- **Protocol inconsistency:** <<Changing kVp from 120 or using thinner slices alters HU/area and invalidates percentile reporting and serial comparison.>>
- **Image noise:** In small patients with low mA, noise spikes can exceed 130 HU — maintain adequate mAs.
- **Arrhythmia:** Misregistration can falsely create or fragment foci; repeat if needed.
- **Over-/under-segmentation:** Review automated contours; do not "bridge" gaps with noise.
- **Young symptomatic patients:** <<Do not falsely reassure with CAC = 0 — non-calcified plaque and ACS are possible.>>

## Special situations

- **Post-CABG:** CAC scoring is generally not useful; native beds heavily calcified and grafts not assessed.
- **Incidental CAC on non-gated chest CT:** Use qualitative scoring (none/mild/moderate/severe); avoid numeric Agatston.
- **Valvular/aortic calcification:** Describe qualitatively; do not include in CAC.

## Technical pearls

- Keep 120 kVp for scoring — if patient is large, increase mA rather than lowering kVp.
- Small cardiac FOV centred on the heart improves spatial resolution and reduces noise.
- Maintain identical protocol across serial scans for valid comparison.
- For CTCA in heavy calcium: consider 120–140 kVp, soft kernel, iterative/AI reconstruction, narrow collimation, and thin-slab MPRs.

## Escalation triggers

- <<CAC ≥1000, or heavy left main/proximal multivessel calcium — discuss with cardiology; consider alternative testing.>>
- Any CAC in adults <45 years or suspected FH — recommend lipid evaluation and preventive cardiology referral.
- Symptomatic patient with severe CAC and typical angina or high-risk features — early cardiology input; do not rely on CAC alone.

## Key Take-Aways

- CAC scoring uses a fixed protocol: <<ECG-gated, non-contrast, 120 kVp, 2.5–3.0 mm slices, with lesions defined as ≥130 HU and ≥1.0 mm².>>
- Agatston categories <<(0; 1–99; 100–399; ≥400; ≥1000)>> stratify risk; <<≥75th percentile for age/sex/ethnicity indicates elevated burden>>.
- In symptomatic patients, CTCA remains first-line — <<CAC alone must not be used to rule out disease>>, and a score of 0 does not exclude non-calcified plaque.
- <<CAC ≥1000 frequently renders CTCA non-diagnostic — discuss alternative testing with cardiology.>>
- Carefully exclude valvular, annular, aortic wall, pericardial, and stent calcification; misclassification is the commonest scoring error.
- Maintain protocol consistency (120 kVp; 3 mm slices) across serial scans and ensure age/sex percentile reporting using a validated calculator such as MESA.
