CT coronary angiography (CTCA) is a non-invasive test with high negative predictive value for excluding coronary artery disease, and is now first-line in the UK for stable chest pain in patients without known CAD. This chapter gives a clinically oriented overview of patient selection, preparation, acquisition, interpretation and safe reporting.

{% tab chapter-1 Clinical Context %}

## What CTCA offers
- Non-invasive, high negative predictive value for excluding CAD
- Direct visualisation of coronary lumen and plaque (calcified and non-calcified)
- Rapid triage in stable chest pain and selected acute presentations
- Additional information on cardiac morphology, great vessels and lungs

## Where it fits in UK practice
- **Stable chest pain**: first-line investigation for suspected CAD in patients without known disease
- **Acute chest pain (low–intermediate risk)**: can exclude ACS when <<ECG is non-ischaemic and high-sensitivity troponin is negative or only minimally elevated without dynamic change>>
- **Special indications**: coronary anomalies, pre-operative planning for non-coronary cardiac surgery, graft patency, limited stent assessment, suspected vasospasm or SCAD after the acute phase

## Indications
- Stable chest pain with intermediate pre-test probability of CAD
- Equivocal, discordant, or uninterpretable functional/ischaemia tests
- Clarification of coronary anatomy (e.g. anomalous origins/course)
- Assessment of bypass grafts
- Pre-procedural planning where coronary anatomy matters
- Low-to-intermediate risk acute chest pain with non-diagnostic ECG/troponin

## Contraindications and cautions

**Absolute**
- Inability to cooperate with breath-hold or lie flat
- Known severe immediate hypersensitivity to iodinated contrast not amenable to premedication
- Pregnancy (unless exceptional indication with MDT agreement)

**Relative**
- <<eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² or acute kidney injury>>
- Uncontrolled tachyarrhythmia or frequent ectopy
- Severe iodine-induced thyrotoxicosis or uncontrolled hyperthyroidism
- Severe asthma or active bronchospasm (limits beta-blocker use)
- Haemodynamic instability or inability to achieve HR control
- Body habitus exceeding scanner limits

## Medication-specific contraindications
- **Beta-blockers**: <<bradycardia <50 bpm, systolic BP <100 mmHg, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block, decompensated heart failure, active severe bronchospasm>>
- **Nitrates**: <<systolic BP <100 mmHg, severe aortic stenosis, HOCM, PDE-5 inhibitor use — sildenafil/vardenafil within 24 h, tadalafil within 48 h>>; recent riociguat; suspected RV infarction
- **Metformin**: continue if eGFR ≥30 and no AKI; <<withhold at time of contrast if eGFR <30 or AKI, and recheck renal function at 48 h before restarting>>

{% tab chapter-1 Preparation and Technique %}

## Pre-scan checklist
- Appropriate indication and consent
- Recent renal function (within 3 months if stable); eGFR ≥30
- Allergy history (contrast, beta-blocker, nitrate)
- Baseline BP and HR; rhythm regular
- Fasting 2–4 hours; caffeine avoided 12–24 hours
- <<18–20G cannula in right antecubital fossa preferred>> (reduces beam-hardening from left brachiocephalic vein)
- Anaphylaxis kit immediately available

## Heart rate control
- <<Target HR ≤60 bpm ideal; ≤65 bpm acceptable for prospective protocols>>
- **Oral metoprolol tartrate**: 50–100 mg PO 1 hour pre-scan if safe
- **IV metoprolol**: 5 mg IV every 2–3 min, titrated to target HR; <<typical total 5–15 mg, max 20 mg>>
- Alternatives: ivabradine 5–7.5 mg PO (start earlier); diltiazem/verapamil if beta-blocker contraindicated
- Defer scan if frequent PVCs; consider retrospective gating if AF with controlled rate

## Vasodilation
- <<Sublingual GTN 400–800 micrograms (1–2 sprays) 2–3 minutes before acquisition>>, unless contraindicated

## Breath-hold coaching
- Practise an 8–12 second breath-hold in gentle inspiration
- Avoid Valsalva — coach the patient to “breathe in normally and hold, don't strain”

## Scanner and gating
- Multidetector CT with ≥64 rows; gantry rotation ≤300 ms
- **Prospective ECG-triggered axial**: lowest dose; best with stable HR ≤60–65
- **High-pitch spiral** (dual-source): very low dose; needs very low, stable HR
- **Retrospective ECG-gated helical**: resilient to arrhythmia, allows functional analysis, higher dose
- kVp by habitus: 70–80 (small), 100 (average), 120 (large)
- Collimation 0.5–0.625 mm; small cardiac FOV centred on heart

## Contrast injection
- Iodinated contrast 300–400 mg I/mL
- <<Iodine delivery rate ≥1.2 g I/s (optimal 1.5–2.0 g I/s)>>
- Volume 60–90 mL (weight/kVp adjusted); 8–12 s injection time
- Saline chaser 30–50 mL at the same flow
- <<Bolus tracking ROI in ascending aorta; trigger at 100–150 HU with 5–7 s delay>>

## Acquisition sequence
1. Verify indication, consent, allergies, renal function
2. Place right antecubital cannula and test patency
3. Apply ECG leads; confirm clean trace
4. Titrate beta-blocker to target HR
5. Administer sublingual GTN and wait 2–3 min
6. Localiser; calcium score if indicated
7. Set bolus tracking and contrast/saline phases
8. Rehearse breath-hold
9. Acquire at diastolic window 65–75% R–R; add systolic 35–45% if motion persists
10. Review for motion; reconstruct additional phases if needed

## Reconstruction
- Slice thickness 0.5–0.75 mm; increment 0.25–0.4 mm
- Medium-soft cardiac kernel; sharper/stent kernel where appropriate
- CPR and orthogonal MPRs for each artery; MIPs for overview; VR only for anatomy/communication, never for stenosis grading

{% tab chapter-1 Interpretation and Reporting %}

## Coronary anatomy
- **LM, LAD** (proximal/mid/distal, diagonals D1, D2)
- **LCx** (proximal/mid/distal, OM1, OM2, LPL)
- **RCA** (proximal/mid/distal, RPL, PDA)
- Intermediate (ramus) if present
- Dominance: right ~80%, left ~10%, co-dominant ~10%

## Plaque characterisation
- Calcified, non-calcified or mixed
- **High-risk (vulnerable) plaque features**:
  - <<Low-attenuation plaque <30 HU core>>
  - <<Positive remodelling index >1.1>>
  - Napkin-ring sign
  - <<Spotty calcification <3 mm length, arc <90°>>
  - Plaque length >5 mm supports risk

## Stenosis grading (diameter reduction)
| Severity | % stenosis |
|---|---|
| Minimal | 1–24% |
| Mild | 25–49% |
| Moderate | 50–69% |
| Severe | 70–99% |
| Occluded | 100% |

## CAD-RADS
- **0**: 0% stenosis
- **1**: 1–24%
- **2**: 25–49%
- **3**: 50–69%
- **4A**: <<70–99% in 1–2 vessels>>
- **4B**: <<Left main ≥50% and/or 3-vessel disease ≥70%>>
- **5**: Total occlusion (100%)

Modifiers: **N** non-diagnostic, **S** stent, **G** graft, **V** vulnerable plaque.

**Suggested management** (align with local MDT)
- 0–2: optimise preventive therapy; no routine ischaemia testing
- 3: consider CT-FFR or functional testing; intensify risk factor treatment
- 4A/4B/5: refer for invasive coronary angiography; urgent if LM/proximal LAD or high-risk symptoms

## Agatston calcium score
- **0**: very low risk; CAD unlikely
- **1–9**: minimal
- **10–99**: mild
- **100–299**: moderate
- **300–999**: severe
- **≥1000**: very severe

## Radiation dose
- Prospective step-and-shoot ~1–5 mSv
- High-pitch spiral often <1–2 mSv
- Retrospective gated with modulation ~5–15 mSv
- Typical adult chest k-factor ≈0.014 mSv/(mGy·cm) — confirm local policy

## Report structure
- Indication and clinical context
- Technical adequacy: HR, rhythm, gating, dose, contrast quality; flag non-diagnostic segments with **N**
- Coronary dominance and variants/anomalies
- Segment-by-segment plaque type, stenosis range and high-risk features
- Bypass grafts and stents with limitations
- Calcium score category if performed
- Significant extracardiac findings
- Conclusion with CAD-RADS category, modifiers and clear management recommendation with urgency

## Escalation triggers
- <<Left main stenosis ≥50%: urgent cardiology discussion for invasive angiography>>
- <<Proximal LAD ≥70%: prompt cardiology referral>>
- <<Multivessel severe disease (≥70% in ≥2 major vessels) or CAD-RADS 4B: cardiology referral>>
- High-risk plaque in proximal segments even with moderate stenosis: expedite prevention and discuss with cardiology
- Non-diagnostic proximal segments supplying large territories: recommend repeat optimised CTCA or alternative test and communicate directly

## Common pitfalls

**Motion artefact**
- Prevent by achieving target HR; use correct phase; try systolic 35–45% if diastolic fails; multicycle recon if available

**Calcium blooming**
- Causes stenosis overestimation; use sharper kernels, thin slices, cross-sectional MPRs; correlate with distal run-off

**Poor opacification**
- Check IV size/position; increase IDR; ensure adequate saline chaser; avoid Valsalva

**Beam-hardening from left brachiocephalic vein**
- Prefer right arm cannulation; adjust arm position if needed

**Small vessels (<2 mm)**
- Do not formally grade stenosis in vessels below clinical relevance

**Stents**
- <<Stents <3.0 mm or heavily calcified may be non-diagnostic for in-stent restenosis>>; use stent-optimised kernels and report limitation explicitly

**SCAD and vasospasm**
- Avoid aggressive beta-blockade if SCAD suspected acutely; correlate clinically; consider invasive angiography

## Contrast reaction management
- **Mild** (urticaria, pruritus): antihistamine; observe
- **Moderate** (bronchospasm, responsive hypotension): oxygen, bronchodilator, IV fluids, antihistamine, steroid
- **Severe/anaphylaxis**: call emergency response; <<IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (1:1000) into anterolateral thigh>>; high-flow oxygen; IV fluids; airway support

## Extravasation
- Stop injection; elevate limb; cold compress; assess volume; surgical review if severe pain or compartment concern

## Beta-blocker adverse effects
- Bradycardia/hypotension: <<atropine 500 micrograms IV, repeat to maximum 3 mg>>; IV fluids; <<glucagon 1–5 mg IV for beta-blocker-refractory cases>>; escalate

## Nitrate adverse effects
- Headache, hypotension: lay flat, reassure, IV fluids if needed, withhold further doses

## On-table decision points
- HR uncontrolled (>70 bpm) with irregular rhythm: switch to retrospective gating or defer
- <<Symptomatic systolic BP <90–100 mmHg after GTN or beta-blocker>>: withhold further dosing, consider fluids, escalate

## Key numbers at a glance
- HR target: ≤60 bpm ideal, ≤65 acceptable
- GTN: 400–800 micrograms SL, 2–3 min pre-scan
- Trigger: 100–150 HU in ascending aorta
- Slice/increment: 0.5–0.75 mm / 0.25–0.4 mm
- IDR: ≥1.2 g I/s (aim 1.5–2.0)
- Prospective windows: diastole 65–75%; systole 35–45%
- PDE-5 washout: sildenafil/vardenafil 24 h; tadalafil 48 h

## Key Take-Aways

- CTCA is first-line in the UK for stable chest pain in patients without known CAD; high negative predictive value makes it ideal for ruling out disease.
- Optimal image quality depends on three pillars: a heart rate ≤60–65 bpm, sublingual GTN before acquisition, and a right antecubital 18–20G cannula delivering an iodine delivery rate ≥1.2 g I/s.
- Always confirm eGFR ≥30, screen for PDE-5 inhibitor use before nitrates, and screen for bradycardia, hypotension, AV block and bronchospasm before beta-blockers.
- Interpret with a structured framework: dominance, segment-by-segment plaque type and stenosis, high-risk plaque features, then assign a CAD-RADS category with N/S/G/V modifiers and a clear management recommendation.
- Escalate urgently for left main ≥50%, proximal LAD ≥70%, or CAD-RADS 4B; communicate non-diagnostic studies in major territories directly.
- Be prepared for contrast reactions and medication adverse effects: anaphylaxis kit available, IM adrenaline 0.5 mg for severe reactions, atropine and glucagon for beta-blocker toxicity.
