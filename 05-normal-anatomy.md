Accurate identification of normal coronary anatomy is the foundation of every CTCA report. A reproducible, segment-based approach lets you confidently describe vessels, recognise benign variants, and flag the rare anomalies that demand urgent escalation.

{% tab chapter-5 Anatomy and Dominance %}

## Aortic root and coronary ostia

- **Right coronary artery (RCA)** arises from the right sinus of Valsalva; **left main (LM)** from the left sinus. The posterior sinus is non-coronary.
- Ostia typically sit at or just below the sinotubular junction, with a perpendicular take-off and a short oval funnel.
- <<Low coronary ostial height (<10–12 mm above the annular plane) increases the risk of coronary obstruction during TAVI.>>

## Coronary dominance

Defined by the origin of the **posterior descending artery (PDA)** at the crux:

- <<Right-dominant: PDA from RCA — approximately 80–85%.>>
- <<Left-dominant: PDA from LCx — approximately 8–10%.>>
- <<Co-dominant: PDA from RCA with posterolateral branches from LCx — approximately 5–10%.>>

**Always state dominance in your report** — it determines which artery supplies the inferior wall and inferoseptum and dictates correct branch labelling.

## Left main coronary artery (LM)

- Diameter ~3.5–5.0 mm; length 5–15 mm (short <5 mm; long >15 mm).
- Usually bifurcates into LAD and LCx; **trifurcation with a ramus intermedius in ≈20–30%**.
- Marked asymmetric tapering or eccentric wall thickening is plaque, not normal.

## Left anterior descending artery (LAD)

- Runs in the anterior interventricular groove; may wrap around the apex.
- Segments:
  - **Proximal**: LM to first major septal/diagonal.
  - **Mid**: between first and second major diagonals/septals.
  - **Distal**: beyond second major branch to the apex.
- Major branches: septal perforators (perpendicular into septum) and diagonals (D1, D2…) over the anterolateral LV.

## Left circumflex artery (LCx)

- Runs in the left AV groove towards the crux.
- Segments:
  - **Proximal**: LM to OM1.
  - **Mid**: OM1 to OM2.
  - **Distal**: beyond OM2.
- Branches: obtuse marginals (OM1, OM2…); in left-dominance, continues as the PDA with PLV branches.

## Right coronary artery (RCA)

- Runs in the right AV groove around the acute margin to the crux.
- Segments:
  - **Proximal**: ostium to first RV marginal.
  - **Mid**: first RV marginal to just before the crux.
  - **Distal**: from the crux to PDA origin.
- Branches (proximal to distal): conus, SA nodal, RV (acute) marginals, AV nodal, PDA, PLV.

## Nodal supply

- **SA node**: <<from RCA in ≈60–65%, from LCx in ≈30–35%, dual supply in ≈5–10%.>>
- **AV node**: <<from RCA in ≈80–90% (right-dominant), from LCx in ≈10–20% (left-dominant).>>

{% tab chapter-5 Technique %}

## Reconstruction

- Thin slices **0.5–0.75 mm**, small FOV centred on the coronary tree.
- Curved planar reformats (CPR) for each epicardial artery.
- Thin-slab MIPs **2–3 mm** along the vessel — avoid thicker slabs that obscure small branches and confuse veins for arteries.

## Windowing

- Default "coronary" window: width 700–900 HU, level 200–300 HU.
- Calcium/stents: widen to width 1000–1500 HU.
- Soft plaque detail: narrow to width 600–700 HU.

## Phase selection

- <<Heart rate <60 bpm: reconstruct at mid-diastole (typically 70–80% R–R).>>
- <<Heart rate >75 bpm or variable rhythm: end-systole (35–45% R–R) usually has less motion.>>
- Choose the best phase per vessel if needed.

## Anatomic anchors

- Identify the **AV (coronary) sulcus** and **anterior/posterior interventricular grooves** on basal short-axis views to predict vessel courses.
- Locate the **crux** (intersection of AV and posterior IV grooves) to determine dominance.

## Recommended reading order

1. Ostia and aortic root.
2. Determine dominance at the crux.
3. LM → LAD (prox→distal) → LCx (prox→distal) with branches.
4. RCA (prox→distal) with branches.
5. Sanity checks: nodal branches, wrap-around LAD, PLV/PDA origin, variants vs anomalies.

## Reference calibres (approximate, body-size dependent)

| Vessel | Proximal | Distal |
|---|---|---|
| LM | 3.5–5.0 mm (length 5–15 mm) | — |
| LAD | 3.0–4.0 mm | 1.5–2.5 mm |
| LCx | 2.5–3.5 mm | 1.5–2.5 mm |
| RCA | 3.0–4.5 mm | 2.0–3.0 mm |

Mild tapering is normal — define disease by wall appearance and plaque, not calibre alone.

{% tab chapter-5 Territories and Variants %}

## AHA 17-segment LV model

Typical territories (vary with dominance and wrap-around):

- **LAD**:
  - Basal: 1 (anterior), 2 (anteroseptal)
  - Mid: 7 (anterior), 8 (anteroseptal)
  - Apical: 13 (anterior), 14 (septal), ±16 and 17 if wrap-around
- **LCx** (right dominance):
  - Basal: 5 (inferolateral), 6 (anterolateral)
  - Mid: 11 (inferolateral), 12 (anterolateral)
  - Apical: ±16 (lateral)
- **RCA/PDA** (right dominance):
  - Basal: 3 (inferoseptal), 4 (inferior)
  - Mid: 9 (inferoseptal), 10 (inferior)
  - Apical: 15 (inferior), ±14 depending on PDA size

## ECG lead correlations (simplified)

- LAD: V1–V4
- LCx: I, aVL, V5–V6
- RCA (right dominance): II, III, aVF

Comment on **wrap-around LAD** or **left dominance** when present — these explain non-standard perfusion patterns.

## Common benign variants

- **Ramus intermedius** (LM trifurcation): ≈20–30%. Label as "ramus" — never as D1 or OM1.
- **Separate conus branch** from the right sinus ("third coronary artery"): ≈30–50%.
- **Separate LAD and LCx ostia** (absent LM): ~1%.
- **Myocardial bridging** (most often mid-LAD): ≈20–40%. Systolic narrowing with normal diastolic calibre is benign.
- **Wrap-around LAD**: frequent; document extent beyond apex.
- **High take-off** ostium (at/above STJ): uncommon, usually benign if no intramural/interarterial course.
- **"Shepherd's crook" RCA**: marked superior angulation of the proximal segment — benign but affects catheter engagement.

## Structured labelling reminders

- State dominance early.
- In **left dominance**, label the PDA as "LCx-PDA".
- Distinguish **OM** (lateral LV free wall) from **PLV** (inferoposterior LV, beyond the crux).

{% tab chapter-5 Pitfalls and Red Flags %}

## Pitfalls

- **Vein vs artery confusion**: the great cardiac vein parallels the LAD/LCx, and the coronary sinus runs in the left AV groove. Veins are lower attenuation in the arterial phase and drain into the coronary sinus — use CPR from the ostia to confirm.
- **OM vs PLV**: OM courses laterally over the LV free wall; PLV runs inferoposteriorly, usually beyond the crux.
- **Overcalling myocardial bridging**: dynamic systolic narrowing with normal diastolic calibre is a benign variant — do not report as stenosis.
- **Ignoring dominance**: always confirm PDA origin before labelling inferior/inferoseptal branches.
- **Thick MIPs**: obscure small branches and promote vein/artery confusion — prefer 2–3 mm slabs aligned to the vessel.
- **Missed separate ostia**: scroll carefully from the annulus up to the STJ to identify a separate conus or SA nodal origin.

## Red flags — escalate immediately

<<Suspected anomalous coronary origin with an interarterial course (between aorta and pulmonary artery), particularly with a slit-like orifice, acute take-off, or intramural segment, must be escalated urgently — these are potentially lethal.>>

Other features requiring senior input and explicit mention in the impression:

- <<Left coronary artery from the pulmonary artery (ALCAPA): look for a dilated RCA and retrograde contrast into the main pulmonary artery.>>
- Single coronary artery with malignant course patterns.
- High-risk myocardial bridge — long or deep, with significant diastolic compression and symptoms.
- Ostial atresia, marked hypoplasia, or proximal coronary aneurysm (consider vasculitis or connective tissue disease).

## Quick reading checklist

- **Ostia**: LM from left sinus, RCA from right sinus; note separate conus or absent LM; no slit-like/acute take-off.
- **Dominance**: state right/left/co-dominant based on PDA origin.
- **LM**: length, bifurcation/trifurcation, normal taper.
- **LAD**: prox/mid/distal, D1/D2, first septal, wrap-around, bridging.
- **LCx**: prox/mid/distal, OM1/OM2, ramus or PLV if present.
- **RCA**: prox/mid/distal, conus, SA nodal, RV marginals, AV nodal, PDA, PLV.
- **Territories**: map via AHA 17-segment model; account for dominance/wrap-around.
- **Technical**: best phase per vessel, appropriate window/MIP thickness, vein/artery differentiation confirmed.

## Key Take-Aways

- <<Always state coronary dominance — right (≈80–85%), left (≈8–10%) or co-dominant (≈5–10%) — based on PDA origin at the crux.>>
- Use consistent segmental landmarks for LAD (first/second major branch), LCx (OM1/OM2) and RCA (first RV marginal, crux) to produce reproducible reports.
- Label a ramus intermedius explicitly — never as D1 or OM1 — and identify a wrap-around LAD when present.
- <<Myocardial bridging with systolic narrowing and normal diastolic calibre is a benign variant — do not report as stenosis.>>
- <<Anomalous origins with an interarterial course, ALCAPA features, or malignant single coronary patterns require urgent escalation regardless of symptoms.>>
- Use thin slices (0.5–0.75 mm), 2–3 mm MIPs and CPRs from the ostia to avoid mislabelling veins as arteries and to confidently distinguish normal anatomy from disease.
