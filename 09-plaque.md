Plaque characterisation extends CTCA beyond stenosis grading by identifying morphological features that predict future acute coronary syndromes. Recognising high-risk plaque (HRP) modifies management even when luminal narrowing is non-obstructive, and underpins modern CAD-RADS 2.0 reporting.

{% tab chapter-9 Plaque Taxonomy %}

## Why it matters

- CTCA features such as low-attenuation plaque and positive remodelling predict ACS independently of stenosis severity.
- HRP identification guides intensification of lipid-lowering therapy, FFRct, and invasive angiography.
- Plaque burden across segments adds prognostic information beyond focal stenosis.

## Composition categories

- **Calcified plaque (CP):** hyperattenuating components <<>350 HU>>; prone to blooming.
- **Non-calcified plaque (NCP):** lower attenuation than the contrast-enhanced lumen, subdivided by HU:
  - Fibrous: <<approximately 60–130 HU>>.
  - Fibro-fatty/lipid-rich: <<approximately 30–60 HU>>.
  - Very low attenuation (lipid-rich/necrotic core): <<<30 HU>>.
- **Mixed plaque:** calcified and non-calcified components within the same lesion.

HU thresholds are reference values derived at 120 kVp and shift with tube voltage, monoenergetic level and reconstruction kernel — interpret in context of your protocol.

## High-risk plaque features

Presence of any feature confers risk; ≥2 features substantially raises risk of ACS.

1. **Low-attenuation plaque (LAP):** <<minimum plaque attenuation <30 HU>>, measured within the non-calcified component, away from lumen and calcium.
2. **Positive remodelling (PR):** <<remodelling index (RI) >1.10>>, where RI = outer vessel area at lesion ÷ average reference outer vessel area. <<Negative remodelling: RI <0.95>>.
3. **Spotty calcification:** <<calcific focus <3 mm in length>> within a non-calcified plaque, typically occupying <90° of vessel circumference.
4. **Napkin-ring sign (NRS):** central low-attenuation core abutting the lumen, surrounded by a higher-attenuation rim on cross-sectional images.

Supportive but less standardised: plaque fissure/ulceration, longitudinal plaque length >5 mm, proximal high plaque burden.

## Indications to emphasise plaque character

- Symptomatic chest pain with non-obstructive CAD (<50%) where risk stratification alters therapy.
- Ambiguous moderate stenoses (50–69%) where morphology can guide need for FFRct or invasive assessment.
- Strong family history or elevated lipoprotein(a).
- Follow-up of known CAD (selected scenarios).

{% tab chapter-9 Technique and Measurement %}

## Acquisition

- **Heart rate control:** aim <<<60 bpm>> if possible; motion blurs non-calcified plaque and can mimic low attenuation.
- **Phase selection:** best motion-free phase, typically end-diastole (70–80% RR); consider end-systole (35–45%) if diastole unstable.
- **Slice thickness/increment:** <<≤0.6 mm thickness with ≤0.4 mm increment>> (50–60% overlap).
- **Kernel:** sharp/medium-sharp cardiac kernel to limit blooming; remain consistent.
- **Iterative reconstruction:** reduce noise while preserving edges.

## Spectral and monoenergetic options

- High-keV VMI (100–120 keV) reduces calcium blooming.
- Low-keV (50–60 keV) boosts iodine contrast but may exaggerate blooming and noise.
- <<For HU-based HRP criteria, use 120 kVp-equivalent images or document the energy level used>>.

## Viewing and post-processing

- Curved planar reformation (CPR) along each vessel centreline.
- Orthogonal MPRs at 0.5–1.0 mm intervals through lesions.
- Initial window width 800–1000 HU, level ~200–300 HU; adjust to avoid mis-estimating non-calcified components.

## Measuring minimum plaque attenuation (HU)

- Use a 1–2 mm² ROI in the darkest area of the non-calcified plaque on the best motion-free phase.
- Keep ROI ≥0.5 mm from luminal contrast and away from calcium edges.
- If ROI SD >20 HU, qualify confidence and repeat on adjacent slices.
- Report the **minimum** HU; <<<30 HU defines LAP>>.

## Measuring remodelling index (RI)

- Cross-section perpendicular to centreline at maximal plaque burden.
- Trace the outer vessel (adventitial) boundary using the fat–adventitia interface.
- Reference = average of disease-free segments proximal and distal of similar calibre.
- RI = lesion vessel area ÷ reference vessel area.
  - <<>1.10 = positive remodelling (HRP)>>.
  - 0.95–1.10 = intermediate.
  - <<<0.95 = negative remodelling>>.

## Spotty calcification and napkin-ring sign

- Measure maximal longitudinal length of calcific foci on CPR; <<<3 mm and embedded in non-calcified plaque = spotty calcification>>.
- Confirm napkin-ring on ≥2 contiguous cross-sections; exclude side-branch ostia and partial volume.

## Documentation

- Record kVp (or monoenergetic keV), kernel, slice thickness and phase whenever reporting HU-based features.
- State any artefacts that limit confidence.

{% tab chapter-9 Reporting and CAD-RADS %}

## Systematic workflow

1. **Image quality check** — heart rate variability, motion, noise, contrast opacification, artefacts. State non-diagnostic segments explicitly.
2. **Segmental survey** — AHA 16/17-segment model along LM, LAD, LCx, RCA.
3. **Classify plaque** — presence, composition, location, relation to branch points.
4. **Grade stenosis** by visual diameter reduction:
   - <<Minimal <25%; Mild 25–49%; Moderate 50–69%; Severe 70–99%; Occluded 100%>>.
   - <<Left main ≥50% is considered severe clinically>>.
5. **Apply HRP criteria** — LAP, RI, spotty calcification, NRS.
6. **Plaque burden** — diffuse vs focal; proximal segment involvement.
7. **Synthesise** with symptoms, calcium score, biomarkers.
8. **Report using CAD-RADS 2.0**.

## CAD-RADS 2.0 essentials

- **CAD-RADS 0–5** based on maximal stenosis.
- Modifiers:
  - **V:** vulnerable plaque — use <<V+ if ≥1 HRP feature present>>.
  - **S:** stent, **G:** graft, **F:** FFRct performed, **E:** extra-cardiac findings.
- Example: *“CAD-RADS 3/V+”* for 50–69% stenosis with HRP.

## Plaque burden scores (optional)

- **Segment Involvement Score (SIS):** count of segments (0–16) with any plaque. <<SIS ≥4 = increased events>>.
- **Segment Stenosis Score (SSS):** each segment 0–3, summed. <<SSS >5 = increased risk>>.
- **Agatston CAC:** <<0; 1–99 mild; 100–399 moderate; ≥400 severe>>. High CAC limits non-calcified plaque assessment — flag reduced confidence.

## Escalation triggers

- **Left main:**
  - <<≥50% stenosis → urgent cardiology discussion; invasive angiography usually indicated>>.
  - 25–49% with LAP <30 HU or PR → consider FFRct or invasive assessment.
- **Proximal LAD:**
  - <<≥70% stenosis → invasive assessment considered>>.
  - 50–69% with HRP → FFRct or invasive angiography.
- **Moderate stenosis (50–69%) elsewhere:** with HRP → FFRct/invasive; without HRP → FFRct if symptoms/ECG suggest ischaemia.
- **Non-obstructive (<50%) with HRP:** intensify high-intensity statin ± ezetimibe/PCSK9; optimise risk factors; cardiology review.
- **Diffuse burden (SIS ≥4 or SSS >5):** flag elevated risk even without focal severe stenosis.
- **Acute chest pain:** culprit lesion with HRP, ulceration and compatible symptoms/ECG/troponin → urgent cardiology assessment.

## Suggested phrasing

- *“Mixed plaque with predominant non-calcified component in the proximal LAD.”*
- *“Low-attenuation plaque with minimum 22 HU; positive remodelling (RI 1.18); spotty calcification present.”*
- *“Maximal stenosis 50–69% in mid RCA; CAD-RADS 3/V+ — consider FFRct or invasive assessment.”*
- *“No high-risk plaque features identified.”*

## Pitfalls

- **Motion artefact:** fuzzy or duplicated edges mimic non-calcified plaque or napkin-ring. Choose best phase; avoid HU measurements on motion-affected slices.
- **Partial volume at lumen edge:** iodine artificially lowers HU and suggests false LAP. Keep ROI ≥0.5 mm from the luminal interface; confirm on adjacent slices.
- **Calcium blooming/beam hardening:** underestimates residual lumen and overcalls mixed plaque. Use sharp kernel, thin slices and high-keV VMI; avoid precise HU within blooming zones.
- **kVp/keV and kernel effects on HU:** <<do not apply the <30 HU LAP threshold to 100 kVp or low-keV images without caution>> — use 120 kVp-equivalent images or document the limitation.
- **Side-branch ostia and venous contamination:** mimic ulcers or low-attenuation pockets. Track on CPR and check for branch continuity.
- **Myocardial bridging:** systolic narrowing may be misread as positive remodelling. Assess in diastole; identify intramyocardial course and muscular halo.
- **Perivascular fat stranding:** is extravascular — plaque lies within the vessel wall. Perivascular fat attenuation indices (FAI) are not routine reporting.
- **Stents:** in-stent disease and neoatherosclerosis are obscured by artefact; qualify confidence and recommend alternative testing if decision-critical.

## Pearls

- Inspect the **proximal 3–4 cm of each vessel** carefully — proximal HRP carries the greatest prognostic weight.
- For **borderline LAP** values (25–40 HU), record both the minimum and average across adjacent slices.
- Use synchronised short-axis, long-axis and CPR views to corroborate napkin-ring signs (require ≥2 contiguous slices).
- **MLA is not robust on CTCA** — rely on % diameter stenosis and consider FFRct or invasive physiology.
- HRP does not automatically mandate invasive angiography — integrate with symptoms, biomarkers, lesion location and stenosis severity.
- HU 30–60 (fibro-fatty) and 60–130 (fibrous) are descriptive only — prognostic value rests on LAP <30 HU and other HRP features.
- When uncertain, describe morphology clearly and recommend correlation rather than forcing a category.

## Checklist before signing the report

- Thin, sharp, motion-free reconstructions reviewed.
- HU measured on 120 kVp-equivalent images.
- Composition and HRP features (LAP, PR, spotty calcification, NRS) explicitly stated.
- Stenosis graded and CAD-RADS 2.0 applied with V modifier where appropriate.
- Proximal and left main disease highlighted with escalation suggestions.
- Limitations and artefacts documented.

## Key Take-Aways

- High-risk plaque features — <<LAP <30 HU, RI >1.10, spotty calcification <3 mm, and napkin-ring sign>> — predict ACS independently of stenosis severity.
- Use 120 kVp-equivalent images, sharp kernels and motion-free phases for any HU-based assessment; document the technical parameters.
- Grade stenosis using standard cut-offs (minimal <25%, mild 25–49%, moderate 50–69%, severe 70–99%, occluded 100%) and apply <<CAD-RADS 2.0 with the V+ modifier when ≥1 HRP feature is present>>.
- <<Left main ≥50% stenosis warrants urgent cardiology discussion>>; moderate stenosis with HRP should prompt FFRct or invasive assessment.
- Plaque burden scores (SIS ≥4, SSS >5) and high CAC (≥400) flag elevated risk even without focal severe stenosis.
- Common pitfalls — partial volume, blooming, motion and bridging — can fabricate or hide HRP; qualify confidence when image quality is reduced.
