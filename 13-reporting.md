A well-structured CTCA report answers the referrer's clinical question, communicates certainty and uncertainty, and proposes the next step. This chapter gives you a practical template, the grading systems and thresholds you must know, and the common pitfalls to avoid.

{% tab chapter-13 Report Structure %}

## Recommended headings

1. **Clinical details** — symptoms, pre-test probability, specific question.
2. **Technique** — scanner, acquisition (prospective/retrospective), kV, ECG-gating, heart rate, medications (β-blocker, GTN), contrast volume/rate, CT-FFR or perfusion if performed, radiation dose (DLP and effective dose).
3. **Image quality** — overall grade, artefacts, non-diagnostic segments and why.
4. **Coronary anatomy overview** — dominance, variants/anomalies.
5. **Coronary findings by segment** — SCCT 18-segment model; plaque type, stenosis grade, high-risk features, CT-FFR.
6. **Calcium score** (if performed) — Agatston, percentile.
7. **Other cardiac findings** — chambers, bridging, pericardium, valves, aortic root.
8. **Extra-cardiac findings** — with management recommendations.
9. **Summary and recommendations** — plain-language synthesis, CAD-RADS, next steps and urgency.

## Technique points to mention

- Heart rate at acquisition and best reconstructed phase (e.g. 75% R-R).
- Adequacy of luminal enhancement (ideal ~300–400 HU).
- Ectopy or AF during scan if affecting quality.
- Limitations: blooming, motion, small stent calibre.
- <<Effective dose ≈ DLP × 0.026 mSv/mGy·cm>>.

## Segment naming (SCCT 18-segment)

- **LM**: 5
- **LAD**: prox 6, mid 7, distal 8; D1 (9), D2 (10)
- **LCx**: prox 11, distal 13; OM1 (12), OM2 (14)
- **RCA**: prox 1, mid 2, distal 3; PDA (4), PLV (15)
- **Ramus intermedius** (16)

Always state right/left/co-dominance and which vessel supplies PDA/PLV.

## Read-before-you-write workflow

1. Review clinical details and risk factors.
2. Check technique, heart rate, phase, enhancement, DLP.
3. Confirm coronary origins and dominance.
4. Work vessel by vessel with CPR and orthogonal cross-sections: LM → LAD → LCx → RCA.
5. For each lesion: plaque type, maximal stenosis, high-risk features.
6. Assess stents/grafts; integrate FFRct if available.
7. Survey cardiac and extra-cardiac findings.
8. Summarise with CAD-RADS and explicit recommendations.
9. Save key images.

## Stenosis grading

Report the most severe grade per segment:

| Grade | Stenosis |
|---|---|
| None | 0% |
| Minimal | <<1–24%>> |
| Mild | <<25–49%>> |
| Moderate | <<50–69%>> |
| Severe | <<70–99%>> |
| Occluded | 100% |

## Clinical thresholds

- <<Obstructive CAD = ≥50% stenosis in a major epicardial vessel (≥2 mm)>>.
- <<Revascularisation thresholds: ≥70% stenosis (non-left main) or ≥50% in the left main>>.
- <<Left main ≥50% or proximal LAD ≥70% are high-risk lesions requiring urgent cardiology review>>.

## Tips for accurate grading

- Use curved planar reformat (CPR) with orthogonal cross-sections.
- Confirm with multiple phases if motion suspected.
- Do **not** overcall calcified segments — blooming exaggerates stenosis.

## Plaque characterisation

Describe plaque type as **non-calcified**, **mixed**, or **calcified**.

## High-risk plaque features

- <<Low attenuation plaque: <30 HU>>
- <<Positive remodelling: remodelling index ≥1.1 (lesion EEM / reference EEM)>>
- Napkin-ring sign (low-attenuation core with higher-attenuation rim)
- <<Spotty calcification: <3 mm>>

One or more high-risk features increases event risk, particularly in proximal segments.

## CT-FFR (if performed)

- Report per-vessel values from proximal to distal.
- <<FFRct ≤0.80 indicates a haemodynamically significant lesion>>.
- Borderline 0.76–0.80 warrants clinical correlation; document the lowest distal value and its location.
- Do not apply FFRct to non-diagnostic segments or heavily stented vessels where the lumen is not assessable.

## Calcium score (Agatston)

| Score | Category |
|---|---|
| <<0>> | None |
| <<1–10>> | Minimal |
| <<11–100>> | Mild |
| <<101–400>> | Moderate |
| <<>400>> | Severe |

Very high CAC (>400–1000) may reduce CTA interpretability and cause overestimation of stenosis from blooming.

## CAD-RADS categories

Base the category on the **worst interpretable lesion**; add **N** if any non-diagnostic segments remain.

| CAD-RADS | Stenosis |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0%, no plaque |
| 1 | <<1–24%>> (minimal) |
| 2 | <<25–49%>> (mild) |
| 3 | <<50–69%>> (moderate) |
| 4A | <<70–99% in 1–2 vessels>> |
| 4B | <<Left main ≥50% or 3-vessel ≥70%>> |
| 5 | <<100% occlusion>> |

## Modifiers

- **N** — non-diagnostic segments present
- **S** — stent present
- **G** — bypass graft present
- **V** — high-risk (vulnerable) plaque features present
- **FFRct** — add result if performed (e.g. FFRct LAD 0.76)

## Management anchors

| Category | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Reassurance; risk factor advice |
| 2 | Preventive therapy and risk factor optimisation |
| 3 | Functional assessment (CT-FFR, stress imaging) or MDT discussion |
| 4A | Invasive angiography or CT-FFR if feasible |
| <<4B–5>> | <<Prompt invasive angiography / Heart Team discussion>> |
| Any V | Intensify secondary prevention; cardiology review |

## Urgent escalation triggers (same/next working day)

- <<Left main ≥50%>>
- <<Proximal LAD ≥70%>>
- <<Suspected triple-vessel severe disease (CAD-RADS 4B)>>
- <<CT-FFR ≤0.80 in a proximal segment correlating with symptoms>>
- Acute aortic or pulmonary vascular emergency

## Stents and grafts

**Stents**: state location, length, diameter (if known), and lumen assessability.

- <<Stents <3.0 mm and overlapping stents are frequently non-diagnostic due to blooming>>.
- Report as: patent (no in-stent restenosis), indeterminate (use N modifier; suggest functional test or invasive angiography), or occluded.

**Grafts**: specify type (LIMA, SVG, radial), course, patency, anastomoses, and distal run-off. Use G modifier; still report native beds.

## Other cardiac findings

- Myocardial bridging: report segment and length; benign in most cases.
- Pericardium, valves (heavy AV calcification, MAC).
- Aortic root / ascending aorta within FOV:
  - <<>40 mm: dilated>>
  - <<≥45 mm: consider specialist follow-up (lower thresholds in bicuspid valve / connective tissue disease)>>
  - <<≥55 mm: typical surgical threshold in a tricuspid aorta>>

## Extra-cardiac findings

- Lung nodules — apply BTS/Fleischner.
- Look for central PE, aortic aneurysm/dissection, pneumothorax.
- Escalate urgently if life-threatening process suspected.

## Common pitfalls

- **Overcalling calcified lesions** — use thin MPR, narrow kernels, cross-sectional views; compare phases.
- **Motion artefact mimicking stenosis** — re-evaluate in best phase; use motion-correction algorithms.
- **Branch misidentification** (ramus vs D1 vs OM1) — track from ostium using CPR.
- **Ignoring non-diagnostic segments** — declare them; add N modifier.
- **Missing anomalous coronary courses** — systematically review origins and interarterial routes.
- **In-stent guessing** — if blooming obscures lumen, state "indeterminate" rather than guess.
- **Myocardial bridging mislabelled as fixed stenosis** — look for "milking" with preserved diastolic calibre.
- **Understating left main disease** — <<any LM ≥50% is high risk and needs urgent discussion>>.
- **Window/level** — for lumen start at WL ~300 HU, WW ~700 HU; adjust to avoid missing non-calcified plaque.
- **Incomplete recommendations** — always provide a management suggestion linked to findings.

## Example impressions

**Normal study**
> CAD-RADS 0. Right-dominant circulation. No plaque or stenosis. No high-risk features. Reassurance and lifestyle optimisation; no further cardiac testing unless symptoms evolve.

**Non-obstructive plaque with high-risk features**
> CAD-RADS 2/V. Mild (25–49%) non-calcified plaque in proximal LAD with low-attenuation focus and positive remodelling. Initiate high-intensity statin and risk factor optimisation; cardiology review if symptoms persist.

**Equivocal moderate lesion with CT-FFR**
> CAD-RADS 3/S. Mixed plaque causing moderate (50–60%) mid-RCA stenosis; mid-LAD stent segments non-assessable (N). FFRct falls to 0.78 distal to the RCA lesion, consistent with flow limitation. Recommend invasive angiography for RCA.

**Severe / prognostically important disease**
> CAD-RADS 4B. Left main ~60% mixed plaque stenosis with low-attenuation component. **Urgent** cardiology review for invasive angiography / Heart Team discussion advised.

**Non-diagnostic study**
> CAD-RADS 2/N. Extensive calcification (Agatston 1150) with blooming causing multiple non-diagnostic segments in proximal LAD and RCA; where assessable, at least mild (25–49%) plaque. Recommend functional assessment or invasive angiography based on clinical risk.

**Bypass graft**
> CAD-RADS 4A/G. Patent LIMA–LAD graft; severe (70–80%) stenosis at SVG–OM anastomosis with reduced distal calibre. Recommend invasive angiography.

## Communicating uncertainty

Be explicit:
> "Assessment of mid RCA is non-diagnostic due to motion despite multiple phases; haemodynamically significant stenosis cannot be excluded. Recommend functional imaging (stress perfusion CMR) or invasive angiography depending on clinical risk and symptoms."

## Sign-off checklist

- Does the report answer the clinical question?
- Are dominance, key lesions, and high-risk segments clearly stated?
- CAD-RADS used with appropriate modifiers?
- Non-diagnostic segments declared?
- Clear management recommendation and urgency?
- Urgent extra-cardiac findings flagged and communicated?
- Key images saved?

## Key Take-Aways

- Structure your report consistently: clinical details → technique → quality → anatomy → segmental findings → calcium score → other cardiac → extra-cardiac → summary with CAD-RADS and recommendations.
- <<Obstructive CAD is ≥50% stenosis; left main ≥50% or proximal LAD ≥70% are high-risk and require urgent cardiology review>>.
- <<FFRct ≤0.80 indicates a haemodynamically significant lesion>>; use it to prioritise moderate stenoses but not in non-diagnostic or heavily stented segments.
- High-risk plaque features (<<low attenuation <30 HU, remodelling index ≥1.1, napkin-ring sign, spotty calcification <3 mm>>) should be reported and trigger intensified prevention.
- Declare non-diagnostic segments using the **N** modifier — never guess across blooming or motion; recommend the appropriate next test.
- Close every report with a plain-language summary, a CAD-RADS category with modifiers, and a clear, time-bound management recommendation.
