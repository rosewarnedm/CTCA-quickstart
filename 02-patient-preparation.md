Good patient preparation is the single biggest controllable determinant of CTCA image quality. It reduces motion artefact, optimises coronary enhancement, and minimises adverse events. This chapter outlines a structured, safety-led approach trainees can apply on the day of every scan.

{% tab chapter-2 Pre-scan Assessment %}

## Why preparation matters

- Primary goals:
  - Achieve a slow, regular heart rate.
  - Ensure coronary vasodilatation and good contrast opacification.
  - Minimise arrhythmia and breath-hold artefact.
  - Mitigate risks from iodinated contrast, drugs, and radiation.
- Consequences of poor preparation: non-diagnostic scans, repeat exposure, avoidable adverse events.

## Indications and contraindications

- Typical indications: stable chest pain with intermediate pre-test probability, anatomical assessment of known CAD, exclusion of anomalous coronaries.
- **Absolute contraindications**:
  - Inability to lie flat or breath-hold safely.
  - <<Known severe iodinated contrast anaphylaxis without adequate premedication and rescue plan.>>
  - <<Pregnancy — CTCA is not first-line; defer unless absolutely essential with senior/obstetric input.>>
- Relative contraindications: AF with high R–R variability, eGFR <30 or AKI, decompensated heart failure, severe asthma/COPD, uncontrolled hyperthyroidism, severe diffuse calcification.

## Patient instructions before attending

- Fasting: no solid food for 2–4 hours; water encouraged.
- Avoid:
  - <<Caffeine for 12–24 hours pre-scan.>>
  - Nicotine for 2–4 hours pre-scan.
  - Decongestants and stimulants on the day.
- **PDE-5 inhibitors — critical to ask and document**:
  - <<Sildenafil or vardenafil: not within 24 hours of GTN.>>
  - <<Tadalafil: not within 48 hours of GTN.>>
- Continue usual cardiac medications unless told otherwise.
- Diabetes: early appointment, bring glucose source, do not omit basal insulin.

## Safety screening

- **Renal function** (check within 3 months if any risk factor):
  - <<eGFR ≥45: proceed routinely.>>
  - <<eGFR 30–44: proceed with caution, ensure hydration, minimise contrast volume.>>
  - <<eGFR <30 or AKI: avoid unless essential; senior sign-off and prophylaxis required.>>
- Contrast allergy: stratify reaction severity; premedicate per local policy for prior mild reactions; senior review and consider alternative test for moderate/severe.
- Breastfeeding: safe to continue after iodinated contrast.
- Thyrotoxicosis (uncontrolled): avoid iodinated contrast where possible.

{% tab chapter-2 Heart Rate Optimisation %}

## Targets

- <<Ideal heart rate ≤60 bpm with regular sinus rhythm.>>
- <<Acceptable ≤65 bpm if rhythm regular and modern motion-correction available.>>
- <<Minimum systolic BP for proceeding with rate control and GTN: ≥100 mmHg (ideally ≥110 mmHg).>>

## Beta-blocker contraindications (screen carefully)

- <<Severe asthma or active bronchospasm.>>
- <<Second- or third-degree AV block without pacemaker.>>
- <<Resting HR <55 bpm or SBP <100 mmHg.>>
- Decompensated heart failure or cardiogenic shock.
- Cautions: insulin-treated diabetes, severe PVD, variant angina.

## Oral premedication (pre-arranged)

- **Metoprolol tartrate 50–100 mg PO 1–2 h pre-scan**:
  - Start with 50 mg; repeat 50 mg after 45–60 min if HR >65 and BP allows.
  - <<Maximum oral metoprolol tartrate pre-scan dose: 100 mg.>>
- Atenolol 50–100 mg PO the night before ± 25–50 mg on the morning.
- Ivabradine (if beta-blocker contraindicated): 5 mg PO BD the day before, then 5–7.5 mg PO 2–3 h pre-scan; sinus rhythm only.

## On-table intravenous rate control

- **Metoprolol IV**:
  - 5 mg slow bolus over 2–3 min; repeat every 3–5 min.
  - <<Maximum IV metoprolol total dose: 15–20 mg.>>
  - <<Hold further doses if SBP <100 mmHg, HR <55 bpm, or AV block develops.>>
- Esmolol: 0.5 mg/kg IV over 1 min; infusion 50–200 micrograms/kg/min for rapid, short-acting effect.
- If beta-blocker contraindicated:
  - Diltiazem IV 0.25 mg/kg (15–20 mg) over 2 min; second bolus 0.35 mg/kg after 15 min if required.
  - Verapamil IV 2.5–5 mg slow; max 10 mg.
  - <<Do not combine IV non-dihydropyridine CCBs with beta-blockers without senior oversight (risk of high-grade AV block and hypotension).>>

## When to switch plan or defer

- Persistent HR >75 bpm or irregular rhythm despite maximal safe therapy: scan with retrospective gating (higher dose) or defer and rebook after outpatient rate control.
- AF with high beat-to-beat variability: often better deferred or alternative test arranged.

{% tab chapter-2 Nitrates and Access %}

## Coronary vasodilatation with GTN

- **Standard dose**: glyceryl trinitrate sublingual spray 400 micrograms (1 spray); 800 micrograms (2 sprays) in larger patients per local protocol.
- **Timing**: <<Administer GTN 2–3 minutes before contrast acquisition.>>
- Always recheck BP and PDE-5 status immediately before administration.

## GTN contraindications

- <<PDE-5 inhibitor within 24 hours (sildenafil/vardenafil) or 48 hours (tadalafil).>>
- <<SBP <100 mmHg.>>
- Severe aortic stenosis or hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy.
- Hypovolaemia.

## IV access and contrast planning

- **Cannula**: <<18–20 G in the right antecubital fossa preferred; avoid small hand veins.>>
- Test injection with saline under power-injector pressure before contrast.
- Contrast:
  - Iodine concentration 320–400 mg I/mL.
  - Typical volume 50–80 mL tailored to patient size and scan duration.
  - <<Flow rate 4.5–6.0 mL/s via power injector.>>
  - <<Saline chaser 30–50 mL at the same flow rate to reduce right heart streak.>>
- Bolus timing:
  - <<Bolus tracking ROI in ascending aorta, trigger at 120–150 HU; scan delay 3–6 s.>>
  - Test bolus 10–20 mL where complex haemodynamics suspected.
- Warming contrast to ~37 °C reduces viscosity.

## Extravasation management

- Stop injection immediately; elevate limb; cold compress; document volume.
- Plastic surgery review if large-volume extravasation or compartment concern.

## Metformin and diabetes

- <<Metformin: continue if eGFR ≥30 and no AKI; withhold for 48 hours after contrast if eGFR <30 or AKI, recheck renal function before restarting.>>
- Warn diabetic patients receiving steroid premedication about hyperglycaemia.
- Anxiolysis (e.g. diazepam 2–5 mg PO 1 h pre-scan) helps cooperation and HR; patient must not drive afterwards.

{% tab chapter-2 Checklist %}

## On-arrival workflow

1. Identity and consent confirmed.
2. Focused history: chest pain status, allergies, asthma severity, recent PDE-5 use, cardiac history, renal risk factors.
3. Observations and rhythm strip: HR, BP, SpO₂.
4. Verify eGFR if risk factors present.
5. Cannulate (18–20 G antecubital); secure and test.
6. Prep skin and apply ECG leads; confirm good R-wave detection.
7. Coach breath-holds (8–10 s comfortable inspiration, no Valsalva).
8. Administer rate control as needed; reassess vitals.
9. Confirm PDE-5 status and BP; administer GTN.
10. Recheck vitals immediately before scan.

## Pre-appointment checklist

- Indication appropriate and alternatives considered.
- Preparation leaflet given (caffeine, nicotine, fasting, PDE-5 warning).
- Renal function checked or arranged if risk factors.
- Allergy history reviewed; premedication plan if needed.
- Rate control strategy agreed (oral vs on-table).
- Diabetes and metformin plan communicated.

## On-day checklist

- Identity, consent, and updated history confirmed.
- PDE-5 status asked and documented.
- HR, BP, rhythm recorded against targets.
- eGFR verified where required.
- 18–20 G antecubital cannula tested.
- ECG leads with reliable R-wave sensing.
- Breath-hold rehearsed.
- Rate control given per protocol; vitals reassessed.
- GTN criteria confirmed and dose administered.
- Emergency kit and trained team available.

## Escalation triggers

- HR >75 bpm or irregular despite maximal safe therapy → senior input; consider retrospective gating or deferral.
- <<SBP <100 mmHg or symptomatic hypotension: withhold further beta-blocker/GTN and treat per protocol.>>
- New chest pain, arrhythmia, or instability: stop elective pathway and escalate to acute team.
- <<eGFR <30 or AKI discovered on the day: do not proceed without senior approval.>>
- Severe prior contrast reaction: ensure full premedication, senior present, consider deferral.

## Emergency preparedness

- Continuous ECG and intermittent BP monitoring throughout.
- Immediate access to: oxygen, suction, anaphylaxis kit (adrenaline, hydrocortisone, chlorphenamine), salbutamol, atropine, IV fluids, vasopressors.
- Staff competent in managing contrast reactions and arrhythmias.

## Key Take-Aways

- Aim for a regular heart rate <<≤60 bpm (acceptable ≤65 bpm)>> before scanning; titrate rate control carefully and stop if <<SBP <100 mmHg or HR <55 bpm>>.
- Always ask about PDE-5 inhibitors and respect the <<24-hour (sildenafil/vardenafil) and 48-hour (tadalafil) intervals>> before giving GTN.
- Stratify renal risk: <<eGFR ≥45 proceed, 30–44 caution, <30 or AKI requires senior decision>>; continue metformin unless eGFR <30 or AKI.
- Use an <<18–20 G antecubital cannula>> with power injection at <<4.5–6.0 mL/s>> and bolus tracking in the ascending aorta at <<120–150 HU>>.
- Screen rigorously for beta-blocker contraindications (severe asthma, high-grade AV block, hypotension, bradycardia) before any oral or IV dose.
- Use structured pre-appointment and on-day checklists — when in doubt, pause, reassess, and escalate early.
