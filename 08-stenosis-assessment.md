Accurate stenosis assessment is the central diagnostic task of CTCA. This chapter outlines a reproducible workflow for grading lesions, applying CAD-RADS v2.0, recognising high-risk plaque, and deciding when to escalate to CT-FFR, functional testing, or invasive angiography.

{% tab chapter-8 Grading and CAD-RADS %}

## Stenosis thresholds (percent diameter stenosis)

- None: 0%
- Minimal: 1–24%
- Mild: 25–49%
- <<Moderate: 50–69%>>
- <<Severe: 70–99%>>
- Occlusive: 100%
- <<Left main is considered significant at ≥50% stenosis, regardless of other vessel involvement.>>

PDS = (1 − MLD/RD) × 100, where reference diameter is the mean of proximal and distal normal segments, matched for natural tapering. Avoid post-stenotic dilated segments as references.

## CAD-RADS v2.0 (stenosis component)

- CAD-RADS 0: 0%
- CAD-RADS 1: 1–24%
- CAD-RADS 2: 25–49%
- CAD-RADS 3: 50–69%
- <<CAD-RADS 4A: ≥70–99% in 1–2 vessels (non-LM).>>
- <<CAD-RADS 4B: LM ≥50% OR 3-vessel disease with ≥70% stenoses.>>
- <<CAD-RADS 5: total occlusion (100%).>>

Modifiers: **N** (non-diagnostic), **S** (stent), **G** (graft), **V** (vulnerable/high-risk plaque), **I** (ischaemia testing result).

## High-risk plaque features

- <<Low-attenuation plaque: <30 HU within the plaque.>>
- <<Positive remodelling: remodelling index >1.1.>>
- Napkin-ring sign: low-attenuation core with a higher-attenuation rim.
- Spotty calcification: <3 mm length and <90° arc.

Presence of any HRP feature warrants the **V** modifier and may influence preventive therapy intensity, even when stenosis is non-severe.

{% tab chapter-8 Workflow and Technique %}

## Acquisition prerequisites

- <<Target heart rate ≤60 bpm with a regular rhythm; use beta-blockade as needed.>>
- Sublingual nitrates unless contraindicated.
- <<Aim for intraluminal attenuation of ~300–450 HU.>>
- Strong breath-hold coaching to minimise motion.

## Reconstructions

- Slice thickness 0.5–0.625 mm with ~50% overlap.
- Multiple phases: diastole (70–80% R–R) for low HR; add systole (35–45%) for RCA or variable HR.
- Standard kernel for general review; sharper kernel for stents and heavy calcium.
- Dual-energy: high-keV (100–130 keV) monoenergetic images reduce calcium and metal blooming.

## Step-by-step assessment

1. **Quality check** — contrast opacification, best phase per vessel, artefact burden.
2. **Dominance and segments** — establish PDA origin; label LM, LAD, LCx, RCA segments.
3. **Survey CPRs** — scroll through curved reformats with linked cross-sectional MPRs.
4. **Define lesion and reference** — measure MLD in a true short-axis plane; average proximal and distal normal references.
5. **Grade stenosis** — assign category; flag LM ≥50% as significant.
6. **Assess HRP** — measure plaque HU, remodelling index, look for napkin-ring and spotty calcification.
7. **Consider CT-FFR** — for moderate, serial, or diffuse disease, or where calcification limits confidence.
8. **Record interpretability** — per segment; apply N modifier as needed.
9. **Summarise** — CAD-RADS category, modifiers, and explicit recommendation.

## Post-processing tips

- Do not measure stenosis on MIP — thin-slab MIP is for tracking course only.
- Starting window/level: W 700–900 / L 200–300 HU.
- For stents: thin sections, sharp kernel, high-keV DECT, true cross-section centred within the stent.

{% tab chapter-8 Special Scenarios %}

## Heavily calcified lesions

- Expect overestimation from blooming; use cross-sections, multiple W/L, and high-keV DECT reconstructions.
- Confirm distal opacification and lumen continuity before calling occlusion.
- If non-diagnostic in clinically critical segments, escalate to CT-FFR (if robust locally), functional testing, or ICA.

## Ostial and bifurcation disease

- Use double-oblique cross-sections orthogonal to each branch.
- Partial volume averaging at ostia (LM, RCA, OM, D1) commonly produces false-positive stenosis.
- At bifurcations, match references to the correct branch and beware of carina calcification mimicking narrowing.

## In-stent restenosis

- <<In-stent restenosis is significant at ≥50% diameter reduction.>>
- Small stents (<3.0 mm), thick-strut older stents, and overlapping stents have high non-diagnostic rates — state this explicitly.
- Evaluate inflow and outflow segments as well as in-stent lumen.
- If indeterminate and symptoms persist, recommend ICA.

## Bypass grafts

- Report graft patency, anastomoses, and run-off separately. Apply S/G modifiers in CAD-RADS as appropriate.

## Myocardial bridging

- Dynamic systolic narrowing with preserved diastolic lumen — assess in **diastole** to avoid overcall.
- Report length and depth; typically benign unless symptomatic.

## CT-FFR

- <<CT-FFR ≤0.80 indicates a haemodynamically significant lesion.>>
- Most useful for intermediate (40–90%, particularly 50–69%) stenoses, serial disease, and where calcification creates uncertainty.

{% tab chapter-8 Pitfalls and Escalation %}

## Common pitfalls

- **Motion mistaken for stenosis** — reconstruct alternative phases (especially systolic for RCA); use motion-correction algorithms where available.
- **Blooming overcall** — prefer cross-sectional MPRs, sharp kernels, and high-keV DECT; never measure stenosis on MIP.
- **Positive remodelling missed** — evaluate outer vessel contour, not just lumen; consider CT-FFR if HRP present with moderate stenosis.
- **Wrong reference diameter** — use both proximal and distal references; avoid post-stenotic dilated segments.
- **Ostial partial volume** — confirm true orthogonal plane across adjacent slices.
- **Mislabelled dominance** — always reconfirm from PDA origin.
- **Serial/diffuse disease underappreciated** — cumulative moderate lesions can be flow-limiting; CT-FFR particularly helpful.
- **Window/level too narrow** — start W 700–900 / L 200–300 HU and adjust.
- **False occlusion calls** — look for trickle flow, bridging collaterals, and delayed contrast if available.

## Escalation triggers

- **CAD-RADS 1–2:** preventive therapy optimisation; no further ischaemia testing in most cases.
- **CAD-RADS 3:** CT-FFR preferred; functional testing if unavailable.
- **CAD-RADS 4A:** CT-FFR if management would change; otherwise MDT vs ICA.
- <<CAD-RADS 4B (LM ≥50% or 3-vessel ≥70%): urgent cardiology referral and ICA.>>
- **CAD-RADS 5:** cardiology referral for ICA; describe CTO features (length, calcification) to aid planning.
- **N modifier with inability to exclude obstructive disease:** CT-FFR (if robust), functional testing, or ICA based on pre-test probability.

## Same-day communication

- <<New LM ≥50% stenosis.>>
- <<Proximal LAD ≥70% stenosis in a symptomatic patient.>>
- Severe multivessel disease with concordant high-risk symptoms.
- Suspicion of acute/subacute occlusion — ensure appropriate ACS pathway.

## Suggested report wording

- "Proximal LAD mixed plaque causing moderate stenosis (50–69%) with positive remodelling (RI 1.2). CAD-RADS 3/V. Consider CT-FFR to assess haemodynamic significance."
- "Left main ostial non-calcified plaque causing approximately 60% stenosis. CAD-RADS 4B. Urgent cardiology referral for invasive angiography is recommended."
- "Heavy calcification renders the proximal RCA non-diagnostic; obstructive disease cannot be excluded (N). Functional assessment or ICA advised."

## Key Take-Aways

- <<Use standardised stenosis thresholds: moderate 50–69%, severe ≥70%, and LM significant at ≥50%.>>
- Apply CAD-RADS v2.0 with appropriate modifiers (N, S, G, V, I) and always pair the category with an explicit management recommendation.
- <<CT-FFR with a threshold of ≤0.80 is the preferred adjunct for intermediate or complex anatomy.>>
- Recognise and report high-risk plaque features — <30 HU plaque, remodelling index >1.1, napkin-ring sign, and spotty calcification — even when stenosis is non-severe.
- Avoid artefactual overcalls from calcium, stents, motion, and ostia by relying on cross-sectional MPRs, sharp kernels, and high-keV DECT reconstructions rather than MIPs.
- Escalate promptly for LM disease, proximal severe stenoses, multivessel severe disease, and any non-diagnostic critical segment where obstructive CAD cannot be excluded.
