CT coronary angiography acquires far more than the coronary tree. A disciplined non-coronary review of the pericardium, myocardium, chambers, valves, aortic root, pulmonary vessels and devices identifies clinically actionable findings, explains symptoms, and changes management. This chapter provides a structured checklist, key thresholds, and reporting cues.

{% tab chapter-10 Review Strategy %}

## Technical set-up

- Slice thickness ≤0.6–0.75 mm with small FOV cardiac reconstruction.
- Primary phase: mid-diastolic (70–80% R–R). If motion present, reconstruct adjacent phases (40–50% systole and 60–95% diastole) for valves and root.
- Build standard cardiac planes: 4-chamber, 2-chamber, short-axis stack, LVOT, RVOT, aortic root/valve plane.

## Windowing

- Soft tissue/cardiac: WL 100–200, WW 700–800 HU.
- Bone/calcium: WL 300–500, WW 1500–2000 HU for valves and pericardium.
- Lung windows for pericardial calcification and fat-containing lesions.

## 10-point non-coronary checklist

1. **Pericardium** – effusion, thickening, calcification, constrictive features.
2. **Myocardium** – hypertrophy, thinning/scar, non-compaction, focal fat, aneurysm.
3. **LV cavity** – size, apical thrombus, papillary muscles, trabeculation.
4. **RV and pulmonary circulation** – RV size/wall, MPA calibre, RVOT.
5. **Atria** – LA size, LAA filling defects, RA, interatrial septum.
6. **Valves and apparatus** – morphology, calcification, MAC, prostheses.
7. **Aortic root and proximal ascending aorta** – diameters, morphology.
8. **Pulmonary and systemic veins** – variants, persistent LSVC, azygos continuation.
9. **Masses and thrombus** – location, attenuation, enhancement.
10. **Devices and prior interventions** – leads, occluders, conduits.

## Practical tips

- Build a 4-chamber and short-axis view set on every case to train pattern recognition.
- Compare arterial and delayed phases side-by-side when assessing for thrombus.
- Always document what cannot be assessed, e.g. *"Valve function cannot be assessed on this single mid-diastolic phase."*

## Pericardium

### Effusion grading (maximal diastolic separation)

- <<Small: <10 mm; Moderate: 10–20 mm; Large: >20 mm.>>
- <<Simple/serous fluid <10 HU; exudative/haemorrhagic >20–30 HU.>>

### Thickening and calcification

- <<Pericardial thickening >2 mm is abnormal.>>
- Calcification may be focal (AV grooves, right heart border) or circumferential.

### CT features of constriction

- Pericardial thickening/calcification, tubular ventricles, biatrial enlargement.
- IVC and hepatic vein dilatation with reflux of contrast into hepatic veins.
- Recommend echocardiography ± CMR for haemodynamic confirmation.

### Pitfalls

- Pericardial recesses (transverse, superior) mimic effusion – follow continuity with pericardial space.
- Epicardial fat necrosis: ovoid fat-attenuation lesion with stranding; self-limited.

## Myocardial assessment

### Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

- <<HCM diagnosis: maximal LV wall thickness ≥15 mm (≥13 mm in first-degree relatives of affected patients).>>
- Distribution: asymmetric septal commonest; assess apex, papillary muscles, LVOT obstruction.
- Recommend echo for gradients and CMR for tissue characterisation.

### Scar, aneurysm and thrombus

- <<LV wall thinning <6 mm suggests chronic infarct/scar.>>
- LV thrombus: mural low-attenuation filling defect, often apical, non-enhancing.
- <<Suspected LV thrombus on CTCA should be confirmed with delayed-phase CT (5–10 min), echo with contrast, or CMR before management decisions.>>

### Non-compaction

- <<End-diastolic non-compacted to compacted ratio >2.3 supports LV non-compaction; confirm with CMR.>>

## Right heart and pulmonary circulation

- <<RV:LV basal diameter ratio >1.0 indicates RV enlargement.>>
- <<RV free wall thickness >5 mm in diastole indicates RV hypertrophy.>>
- Pulmonary hypertension surrogates:
  - <<Main pulmonary artery >29 mm suggests PH; >34 mm supports severe disease.>>
  - <<MPA/ascending aorta ratio >1.0 supports PH in non-elderly adults.>>
- ARVC suspicion: RV dilatation, regional bulging, wall thinning, intramyocardial fat (free wall/outflow) – recommend dedicated CMR.

## Atria and septum

- LA enlargement: AP diameter >40 mm; recommend echo for volume.
- Lipomatous hypertrophy of interatrial septum: dumbbell fat thickening sparing fossa ovalis, typically >20 mm.
- Sinus venosus defects near SVC–RA junction commonly associate with PAPVR.

## Left atrial appendage

- Single arterial-phase CTCA frequently shows pseudo-filling defects from slow flow.
- <<Never diagnose LAA thrombus from a single arterial-phase acquisition – obtain a delayed phase at 60–90 s or recommend TEE/CMR.>>
- On delayed imaging:
  - <<LAA/LA mean attenuation ratio >0.75 supports slow flow rather than thrombus.>>
  - Thrombus: persistent well-defined hypodensity with minimal change between phases.
- Post-occluder leak: <<peri-device jet width >5 mm is clinically significant.>>

## Aortic valve

### Bicuspid aortic valve

- Sievers classification: type 0 (no raphe), type 1 (single raphe – most often right–left fusion), type 2 (two raphes).
- Strong association with root/ascending aortopathy – assess thresholds in aortic section.

### Aortic valve calcium score (severe AS thresholds)

- <<Severe AS: men Agatston ≥2000 AU; women ≥1200 AU.>>
- <<Moderate AS: men 1000–2000 AU; women 400–1200 AU.>>
- Severe by calcium score → recommend prompt echocardiography and Heart Team review.
- CTCA is **not** reliable for planimetric area or gradients.

### Prosthetic valves

- Metal artefact limits leaflet motion assessment; CT excels at periannular complications.
- Attenuation guide for obstructive masses on prostheses:
  - <<Thrombus mean attenuation typically <90 HU; pannus typically >145 HU; 90–145 HU indeterminate.>>
- Periannular abscess/pseudoaneurysm warrants urgent Heart Team discussion.

## Mitral apparatus

- Document mitral annular calcification: extent, thickness, caseous degeneration (central low attenuation with calcified rim).
- Prolapse is dynamic and not reliably assessed on mid-diastolic CT – recommend echo if leaflet redundancy/elongated chordae suspected.

## Aortic root and proximal ascending aorta

### Measurement technique

- Double-oblique planes orthogonal to vessel axis, inner-edge to inner-edge.
- Sites: annulus, sinuses of Valsalva, sinotubular junction, ascending aorta 2–3 cm above STJ.

### Thresholds

- <<Flag for surveillance: aortic root or ascending aorta ≥40 mm.>>
- Typical surgical thresholds (individualise per guideline):
  - <<Degenerative ascending aorta: ≥55 mm.>>
  - <<Bicuspid aortopathy with risk factors: consider ≥50 mm.>>
  - <<Marfan syndrome: ≥50 mm (lower with rapid growth or strong family history).>>
  - <<Loeys–Dietz syndrome: often ≥45 mm (genotype/centre-specific).>>
- <<Interval growth ≥3 mm/year on consistent technique warrants urgent referral.>>

## Veins and devices

- Persistent left SVC: drains to <<dilated coronary sinus (>10 mm).>>
- Interrupted IVC with azygos continuation: enlarged azygos vein; flag for line/device planning.
- Pacing leads: confirm intracardiac course, exclude perforation beyond epicardial surface.

{% tab chapter-10 Reporting and Escalation %}

## Reporting structure

- Place non-coronary findings under a clear heading after the coronary report.
- Lead with clinically significant items and explicit recommendations.
- Use precise measurements with thresholds for context.

## Sample phrasing

- **Pericardium:** *"Moderate circumferential pericardial effusion (max 15 mm, ~5 HU). No CT features of constriction. Recommend echocardiographic correlation."*
- **LAA:** *"Low-attenuation filling defect within the LAA on arterial phase. Recommend 60–90 s delayed acquisition or TEE to exclude thrombus before cardioversion."*
- **AV calcium:** *"Non-contrast aortic valve calcium score 2,350 AU (male) – consistent with severe aortic stenosis. Recommend prompt echocardiographic assessment."*
- **Aortic root:** *"Ascending aorta 48 mm (inner–inner) 2 cm above STJ. With known bicuspid valve, advise cardiology review and surveillance per BAV aortopathy guidance."*
- **PA/RV:** *"MPA 35 mm, PA/Ao ratio 1.1, RV:LV >1 – features compatible with pulmonary hypertension. Recommend echocardiography."*

## Same-day / urgent escalation

- <<Large pericardial effusion with chamber compression features or haemorrhagic attenuation – discuss urgently with cardiology for tamponade risk.>>
- <<Persistent LAA or LV thrombus on delayed imaging – urgent cardiology review for anticoagulation.>>
- <<Suspected prosthetic valve obstruction or periannular abscess/pseudoaneurysm – urgent Heart Team referral.>>
- <<Ascending aorta ≥55 mm (or syndrome-specific lower threshold) or interval growth ≥3 mm/year – urgent cardiothoracic referral.>>
- Incidental central pulmonary embolus identified on cardiac dataset.

## Semi-urgent referral (within weeks)

- <<Severe aortic stenosis by calcium score (men ≥2000 AU; women ≥1200 AU) – prompt echocardiography.>>
- BAV with ascending aorta ≥45–50 mm – patient-specific cardiology review.
- New cardiomyopathy features (HCM, suspected ARVC, LV non-compaction) – CMR and specialist clinic.
- Pulmonary hypertension surrogates with RV enlargement – echocardiography and PH pathway.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

- Motion at the valves/root – reconstruct alternative phases before calling abnormality.
- Beam hardening from dense calcium or prostheses – review with adjusted windows and multiple planes.
- Pseudo-thrombus in LAA from slow flow – mandatory delayed imaging or echo correlation.
- Pericardial recess versus mass or cyst – track continuity with pericardial space.
- Lipomatous fossa ovalis sparing – do not mistake for tumour; fat attenuation (-80 to -30 HU) and dumbbell shape are diagnostic.

## Key Take-Aways

- Apply a 10-point non-coronary checklist on every CTCA – the dataset answers questions beyond the coronaries and incidental findings frequently change management.
- <<Never diagnose LAA or LV thrombus from a single arterial-phase scan; obtain a 60–90 s delayed acquisition or recommend TEE/CMR.>>
- <<HCM (wall thickness ≥15 mm), severe AS by valve calcium score (men ≥2000 AU; women ≥1200 AU) and ascending aorta ≥55 mm are findings that demand explicit recommendations and referral pathways.>>
- <<Pulmonary hypertension surrogates – MPA >29 mm, PA/Ao >1.0, RV:LV >1.0 – should be flagged with an echo recommendation.>>
- For prosthetic valves, remember <<thrombus is typically <90 HU and pannus >145 HU>>, and any periannular collection warrants urgent Heart Team discussion.
- Report what you can and cannot assess – CTCA is a single-phase, mostly mid-diastolic dataset; valve function and shunts require dedicated imaging.
