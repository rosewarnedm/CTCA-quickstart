CT coronary angiography is a thoracic CT with cardiac-optimised timing but a limited z-axis. Clinically important non-cardiac findings occur in a substantial minority of studies and some are urgent. A structured, efficient extracardiac review using wide field-of-view reconstructions is an essential part of every CTCA report.

## Why extracardiac review matters

- Incidental but clinically important pathology is found in <<up to 30–40% of CTCA studies>>; a small but real proportion require urgent action (pulmonary embolus, aortic syndromes, large pericardial effusion, lung cancer).
- CTCA often answers more than one clinical question in breathless or chest-pain patients.
- Your report directs onward investigation — be clear, succinct, and aligned to local pathways.

## Technical principles

- **Reconstructions**: Cardiac small FOV for coronaries; wide FOV thoracic reconstruction with mediastinal and lung kernels for the rest.
- **Windowing**
  - Mediastinum: W ~350–450, L ~30–50.
  - Lungs: W ~1500, L ~−600.
  - Bone: W ~2000, L ~300–400.
- **Slice thickness and MIP**: Thin (0.5–0.75 mm) for pericardium/small nodules; 5–10 mm MIP slabs on lung kernel to screen for nodules.
- **ECG gating effects**: Prospective gating gives step artefacts — beware overcalling dissection or nodules near the heart.
- **Contrast timing**: Optimised for coronaries; pulmonary arteries opacify variably. <<CTCA is not a substitute for CTPA when PE is clinically suspected.>>

## Systematic review checklist

Work top-to-bottom, inside-to-outside, alternating lung and mediastinal windows.

1. **Lungs** — nodules, consolidation, ground-glass, bronchiectasis, emphysema, pneumothorax.
2. **Pleura** — effusion, plaques, thickening, pneumothorax.
3. **Mediastinum and hila** — nodes, thyroid, trachea, oesophagus.
4. **Great vessels and pericardium** — aorta, pulmonary arteries, pericardial space.
5. **Upper abdomen** — liver, adrenals, kidneys, spleen.
6. **Chest wall, breasts, axillae, bones** — masses, nodes, lytic/sclerotic lesions, fractures.
7. **Lines and devices** — grafts, valves, leads, sternal wires.

## Practical reporting

- State that extracardiac structures have been reviewed within the imaged field.
- Quote measurements and tie recommendations to a named guideline (BTS, Fleischner, ACR).
- Record important negatives where clinically relevant.
- State limitations (e.g. "lung apices not included", "arterial timing suboptimal for PE").

## Solid pulmonary nodules

Measure on thin lung kernel as the average of long- and short-axis diameter; use volumetry where available. Management follows local policy — UK practice typically uses BTS (volumetry-based) or Fleischner 2017 (diameter-based).

### BTS thresholds (volumetry preferred)

- <<Volume <80 mm³ (≈ ≤4.3 mm): no follow-up.>>
- **Volume 80–300 mm³ (≈ >4.3–8 mm)**: CT surveillance with volume doubling time (VDT).
  - <<VDT <400 days: high risk → lung MDT ± PET-CT/tissue.>>
  - VDT 400–600 days: indeterminate → continue surveillance.
  - VDT >600 days: likely benign → discharge.
- <<Volume >300 mm³ (≈ >8 mm): risk stratify (e.g. Brock model); if risk ≥10% or suspicious morphology → PET-CT and MDT.>>

### Fleischner 2017 — solid nodules (adults ≥35 years, non-screening, immunocompetent)

| Size | Single nodule | Multiple nodules |
|---|---|---|
| <6 mm | No routine follow-up | No routine follow-up |
| 6–8 mm | <<CT at 6–12 months; consider 18–24 months>> | CT at 3–6 months; consider 18–24 months |
| >8 mm | <<CT at ~3 months, PET-CT and/or tissue sampling>> | Manage by most suspicious nodule |

### Fleischner 2017 — subsolid nodules

- **Pure ground-glass <6 mm**: no routine follow-up.
- **Pure ground-glass ≥6 mm**: CT at 6–12 months, then every 2 years until 5 years.
- <<Part-solid ≥6 mm: CT at 3–6 months; if persistent, annual CT until 5 years; solid component >6 mm → consider PET-CT/tissue diagnosis.>>

### Escalation triggers

- Mass or nodule >8 mm with suspicious morphology.
- <<Part-solid nodule with solid component >6 mm.>>
- Rapid growth (VDT <400 days).
- Associated lymphadenopathy or chest wall invasion.

## Other parenchymal disease

- **Infection/inflammation**: consolidation, tree-in-bud, lobar collapse — correlate clinically.
- **Interstitial lung disease**: basal subpleural reticulation, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing → recommend non-contrast HRCT; refer to ILD MDT if fibrosing features.
- **Emphysema**: describe pattern and severity.
- **Bronchiectasis**: bronchus-to-artery ratio >1, lack of tapering, visibility within 1 cm of pleura.
- **Pneumothorax**: <<tension features (mediastinal shift, flattened diaphragm) require immediate clinical alert.>>

## Pleura

- Effusion: small/moderate/large, simple vs high attenuation.
- Plaques and thickening — asbestos exposure.
- <<Nodular or circumferential pleural thickening suggests malignancy → recommend dedicated contrast-enhanced chest CT.>>

## Thoracic aorta

Measure inner-to-inner in true axial planes perpendicular to the vessel at: aortic root, sinotubular junction, mid-ascending, arch, proximal descending.

### Diameter terminology and thresholds

- Normal mid-ascending: up to ~36–38 mm.
- **Ectasia**: 40–44 mm.
- <<Aneurysm: ≥45 mm.>>
- <<Elective ascending aortic repair threshold: ≥55 mm.>>
- <<Descending thoracic aorta repair threshold: ≥60 mm (TEVAR often considered ≥55–60 mm).>>
- <<Bicuspid aortic valve with risk factors/family history: consider repair at ≥50 mm.>>
- <<Marfan/Loeys–Dietz: 45–50 mm depending on genotype.>>
- <<Concerning growth: ≥3 mm/year → aortic clinic referral.>>

### Acute aortic syndrome on CTCA

Red flags:
- Intimal flap.
- Displaced intimal calcification.
- <<Intramural haematoma — crescentic high attenuation (60–70 HU), no enhancement.>>
- Focal contrast outpouching (penetrating ulcer).

<<If acute aortic syndrome is suspected, arrange urgent gated CT aorta and contact the aortic team — do not rely on CTCA alone.>>

## Pulmonary arteries

- **PE signs**: intraluminal low-attenuation defect outlined by contrast; saddle/lobar defects higher risk. Beware mixing artefact near RVOT/main PA.
- **RV strain markers**:
  - <<RV:LV end-diastolic diameter ratio >1.>>
  - Interventricular septal bowing towards LV.
  - Contrast reflux into IVC/hepatic veins.
- **Pulmonary hypertension markers**:
  - <<Main PA diameter >29 mm.>>
  - <<PA:ascending aorta diameter ratio >1.>>

If convincing PE on CTCA: immediate discussion; dedicated CTPA may still be needed for full burden assessment.

## Pericardium

Maximal thickness in diastole:

- **Small**: <10 mm.
- **Moderate**: 10–20 mm.
- **Large**: >20 mm.

<<Tamponade indicators: RA or RV diastolic collapse, dilated IVC with reduced variability, contrast reflux into hepatic veins — urgent cardiology review.>>

<<Pericardial thickening >3–4 mm and calcification suggest chronic/constrictive pericarditis.>>

## Mediastinum and hila

### Lymph nodes

- <<Mediastinal/hilar short-axis >10 mm is enlarged; cardiophrenic threshold often >8 mm.>>
- Morphology matters: round shape, loss of fatty hilum, necrosis, clustering.
- Unexplained adenopathy ≥10 mm SA → recommend dedicated chest CT ± PET-CT.

### Thyroid (ACR Incidental Thyroid guidance)

- <<Age <35 years: ultrasound if nodule ≥10 mm or suspicious features.>>
- <<Age ≥35 years: ultrasound if nodule ≥15 mm or suspicious features.>>
- Suspicious CT features: irregular margins, markedly hypoattenuating solid mass, calcified nodes.

### Oesophagus and hiatus

- Hiatus hernia — sliding vs para-oesophageal.
- <<Oesophageal wall thickening >5 mm or mass → recommend OGD.>>
- Para-oesophageal hernia with volvulus/strangulation signs → urgent surgical review.

## Upper abdomen (within coverage)

### Liver

- **Simple cyst**: water attenuation (−10 to +20 HU), sharp margins, no enhancement — no action.
- Indeterminate lesion >1 cm → ultrasound or multiphasic liver CT/MRI per clinical context.

### Adrenals

- Nodules ≤10 mm: usually no further action unless suspicious.
- <<Non-contrast HU ≤10 indicates a lipid-rich adenoma.>>
- <<Adrenal lesion ≥4 cm, heterogeneous, or irregular → dedicated adrenal imaging and biochemical work-up.>>
- 1–4 cm indeterminate → adrenal protocol CT or MRI per local pathway.

### Kidneys and spleen

- Simple renal cyst: no action.
- Solid renal lesion or complex cyst → renal ultrasound or contrast renal CT/MRI (Bosniak cannot be assigned on CTCA).
- Splenomegaly: report if craniocaudal length >125 mm.

## Breasts, axillae and bones

- **Suspicious breast findings**: spiculated/irregular mass, architectural distortion, skin thickening, suspicious axillary nodes (<<cortical thickness >3 mm or loss of fatty hilum>>).
- <<Suspicious breast mass or unexplained axillary adenopathy → 2-week-wait symptomatic breast clinic referral.>>
- **Vertebral compression fracture**: <<>20% loss of vertebral body height or >4 mm reduction vs adjacent levels is significant>>; assess for retropulsion.
- Lytic/sclerotic lesions: comment on number and distribution; recommend targeted imaging (bone scan, PET-CT, MRI) if new/suspicious.
- Sternotomy changes: expected wires; report lucency, displacement or soft-tissue gas (suggests non-union/osteomyelitis).

## Common pitfalls

- **Mixing artefact** in pulmonary arteries and SVC can mimic PE — confirm in orthogonal planes; compare with contralateral vessels.
- **Step artefact** from prospective ECG gating can mimic dissection or nodules — review adjacent slices and use MIPs.
- **Partial FOV**: state limitations (e.g. "lung apices not included").
- **Beam hardening** from dense SVC contrast streaks across mediastinum/RUL — use iterative reconstruction and corroborate on unaffected planes.
- **Overcalling thyroid/breast findings** — CT is not tailored for characterisation; escalate when thresholds are met, not before.
- **Aortic mis-measurement** — use true axial planes perpendicular to the vessel centreline; avoid oblique calipers.
- **Inconsistent nodule measurement** — lung kernel, thin slices, average of long/short axes; same technique on follow-up.

## Immediate escalation triggers

- <<Suspected acute aortic syndrome — urgent gated CT aorta and aortic team discussion.>>
- <<Central/lobar pulmonary embolus with right heart strain or haemodynamic instability — urgent clinical alert.>>
- <<Large pericardial effusion with tamponade features — urgent cardiology review.>>
- <<Tension pneumothorax — immediate clinical alert.>>
- Rapidly progressive lung mass or haemoptysis with focal mass — urgent lung cancer pathway referral.

## Example reporting phrases

- **Nodule**: "Right upper lobe 7 mm solid nodule, smooth margins, no calcification. Fleischner 2017: CT at 6–12 months."
- **Aorta**: "Mid-ascending aorta 44 mm (ectasia). Recommend surveillance; refer if growth ≥3 mm/year or approaching 45–50 mm depending on risk."
- **Pericardium**: "Moderate circumferential pericardial effusion (max 14 mm); no CT features of tamponade. Cardiology follow-up recommended."
- **Thyroid**: "Left thyroid lobe nodule 16 mm. Age 62. Recommend thyroid ultrasound."

## Key Take-Aways

- Load wide FOV lung and mediastinal reconstructions for every CTCA and sweep top-to-bottom on lung windows before reviewing the heart.
- Measure and record: nodules (mm or mm³), aortic diameters, pericardial fluid (mm), lymph node short-axis, main PA and PA:Ao ratio.
- Tie recommendations to named thresholds (BTS, Fleischner, ACR) — it reassures clinicians and reduces unnecessary follow-up.
- Aneurysm threshold is ≥45 mm; ascending repair typically ≥55 mm, descending ≥60 mm, lower (45–50 mm) for heritable aortopathies.
- CTCA is not a substitute for CTPA — if PE is suspected clinically, arrange a dedicated study.
- For suspected acute aortic syndrome, tamponade, central PE with strain, or tension pneumothorax, escalate immediately and arrange the right dedicated investigation rather than over- or under-calling on CTCA.
